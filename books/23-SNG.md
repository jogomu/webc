# The Song of Solomon

*[World English Bible (Catholic)](README.md) — Text version: signed by eBible.org 2026-07-24 (pulled 2026-07-26).*

## Song of Solomon 1

<sup>1</sup> The Song of songs, which is Solomon’s.

**Beloved**

<sup>2</sup> Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth;  
&emsp;for your love is better than wine.  
<sup>3</sup> Your oils have a pleasing fragrance.  
&emsp;Your name is oil poured out,  
&emsp;therefore the virgins love you.  
<sup>4</sup> Take me away with you.  
&emsp;Let’s hurry.  
&emsp;The king has brought me into his rooms.

**Friends**

We will be glad and rejoice in you.  
&emsp;We will praise your love more than wine!

**Beloved**

They are right to love you.  
<sup>5</sup> I am dark, but lovely,  
&emsp;you daughters of Jerusalem,  
&emsp;like Kedar’s tents,  
&emsp;like Solomon’s curtains.  
<sup>6</sup> Don’t stare at me because I am dark,  
&emsp;because the sun has scorched me.  
My mother’s sons were angry with me.  
&emsp;They made me keeper of the vineyards.  
&emsp;I haven’t kept my own vineyard.  
<sup>7</sup> Tell me, you whom my soul loves,  
&emsp;where you graze your flock,  
&emsp;where you rest them at noon;  
&emsp;for why should I be as one who is veiled  
&emsp;beside the flocks of your companions?

**Lover**

<sup>8</sup> If you don’t know, most beautiful among women,  
&emsp;follow the tracks of the sheep.  
&emsp;Graze your young goats beside the shepherds’ tents.

<sup>9</sup> I have compared you, my love,  
&emsp;to a steed in Pharaoh’s chariots.  
<sup>10</sup> Your cheeks are beautiful with earrings,  
&emsp;your neck with strings of jewels.

**Friends**

<sup>11</sup> We will make you earrings of gold,  
&emsp;with studs of silver.

**Beloved**

<sup>12</sup> While the king sat at his table,  
&emsp;my perfume spread its fragrance.  
<sup>13</sup> My beloved is to me a sachet of myrrh,  
&emsp;that lies between my breasts.  
<sup>14</sup> My beloved is to me a cluster of henna blossoms  
&emsp;from the vineyards of En Gedi.

**Lover**

<sup>15</sup> Behold,[^1] you are beautiful, my love.  
&emsp;Behold, you are beautiful.  
&emsp;Your eyes are like doves.

**Beloved**

<sup>16</sup> Behold, you are beautiful, my beloved, yes, pleasant;  
&emsp;and our couch is verdant.

**Lover**

<sup>17</sup> The beams of our house are cedars.  
&emsp;Our rafters are firs.

## Song of Solomon 2

**Beloved**

<sup>1</sup> I am a rose of Sharon,  
&emsp;a lily of the valleys.

**Lover**

<sup>2</sup> As a lily among thorns,  
&emsp;so is my love among the daughters.

**Beloved**

<sup>3</sup> As the apple tree among the trees of the wood,  
&emsp;so is my beloved among the sons.  
I sat down under his shadow with great delight,  
&emsp;his fruit was sweet to my taste.  
<sup>4</sup> He brought me to the banquet hall.  
&emsp;His banner over me is love.  
<sup>5</sup> Strengthen me with raisins,  
&emsp;refresh me with apples;  
&emsp;for I am faint with love.  
<sup>6</sup> His left hand is under my head.  
&emsp;His right hand embraces me.  
<sup>7</sup> I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem,  
&emsp;by the roes, or by the hinds of the field,  
&emsp;that you not stir up, nor awaken love,  
&emsp;until it so desires.

<sup>8</sup> The voice of my beloved!  
&emsp;Behold, he comes,  
&emsp;leaping on the mountains,  
&emsp;skipping on the hills.  
<sup>9</sup> My beloved is like a roe or a young deer.  
&emsp;Behold, he stands behind our wall!  
He looks in at the windows.  
&emsp;He glances through the lattice.

<sup>10</sup> My beloved spoke, and said to me,  
&emsp;“Rise up, my love, my beautiful one, and come away.  
<sup>11</sup> For behold, the winter is past.  
&emsp;The rain is over and gone.  
<sup>12</sup> The flowers appear on the earth.  
&emsp;The time of the singing has come,  
&emsp;and the voice of the turtledove is heard in our land.  
<sup>13</sup> The fig tree ripens her green figs.  
&emsp;The vines are in blossom.  
&emsp;They give out their fragrance.  
Arise, my love, my beautiful one,  
&emsp;and come away.”

**Lover**

<sup>14</sup> My dove in the clefts of the rock,  
&emsp;in the hiding places of the mountainside,  
&emsp;let me see your face.  
&emsp;Let me hear your voice;  
&emsp;for your voice is sweet and your face is lovely.  
<sup>15</sup> Catch for us the foxes,  
&emsp;the little foxes that plunder the vineyards;  
&emsp;for our vineyards are in blossom.

**Beloved**

<sup>16</sup> My beloved is mine, and I am his.  
&emsp;He browses among the lilies.  
<sup>17</sup> Until the day is cool, and the shadows flee away,  
&emsp;turn, my beloved,  
&emsp;and be like a roe or a young deer on the mountains of Bether.

## Song of Solomon 3

<sup>1</sup> By night on my bed,

&emsp;I sought him whom my soul loves.  
&emsp;I sought him, but I didn’t find him.  
<sup>2</sup> I will get up now, and go about the city;  
&emsp;in the streets and in the squares I will seek him whom my soul loves.  
&emsp;I sought him, but I didn’t find him.  
<sup>3</sup> The watchmen who go about the city found me;  
&emsp;“Have you seen him whom my soul loves?”  
<sup>4</sup> I had scarcely passed from them,  
&emsp;when I found him whom my soul loves.  
I held him, and would not let him go,  
&emsp;until I had brought him into my mother’s house,  
&emsp;into the room of her who conceived me.

<sup>5</sup> I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem,  
&emsp;by the roes, or by the hinds of the field,  
&emsp;that you not stir up nor awaken love,  
&emsp;until it so desires.

<sup>6</sup> Who is this who comes up from the wilderness like pillars of smoke,  
&emsp;perfumed with myrrh and frankincense,  
&emsp;with all spices of the merchant?  
<sup>7</sup> Behold, it is Solomon’s carriage!  
&emsp;Sixty mighty men are around it,  
&emsp;of the mighty men of Israel.  
<sup>8</sup> They all handle the sword, and are expert in war.  
&emsp;Every man has his sword on his thigh,  
&emsp;because of fear in the night.

<sup>9</sup> King Solomon made himself a carriage  
&emsp;of the wood of Lebanon.  
<sup>10</sup> He made its pillars of silver,  
&emsp;its bottom of gold, its seat of purple,  
&emsp;the middle of it being paved with love,  
&emsp;from the daughters of Jerusalem.  
<sup>11</sup> Go out, you daughters of Zion, and see King Solomon,  
&emsp;with the crown with which his mother has crowned him,  
&emsp;in the day of his weddings,  
&emsp;in the day of the gladness of his heart.

## Song of Solomon 4

**Lover**

<sup>1</sup> Behold, you are beautiful, my love.

&emsp;Behold, you are beautiful.  
Your eyes are like doves behind your veil.  
&emsp;Your hair is as a flock of goats,  
&emsp;that descend from Mount Gilead.  
<sup>2</sup> Your teeth are like a newly shorn flock,  
&emsp;which have come up from the washing,  
&emsp;where every one of them has twins.  
&emsp;None is bereaved among them.  
<sup>3</sup> Your lips are like scarlet thread.  
&emsp;Your mouth is lovely.  
&emsp;Your temples are like a piece of a pomegranate behind your veil.  
<sup>4</sup> Your neck is like David’s tower built for an armory,  
&emsp;on which a thousand shields hang,  
&emsp;all the shields of the mighty men.  
<sup>5</sup> Your two breasts are like two fawns  
&emsp;that are twins of a roe,  
&emsp;which feed among the lilies.

<sup>6</sup> Until the day is cool, and the shadows flee away,  
&emsp;I will go to the mountain of myrrh,  
&emsp;to the hill of frankincense.

<sup>7</sup> You are all beautiful, my love.  
&emsp;There is no spot in you.  
<sup>8</sup> Come with me from Lebanon, my bride,  
&emsp;with me from Lebanon.  
&emsp;Look from the top of Amana,  
&emsp;from the top of Senir and Hermon,  
&emsp;from the lions’ dens,  
&emsp;from the mountains of the leopards.

<sup>9</sup> You have ravished my heart, my sister, my bride.  
&emsp;You have ravished my heart with one of your eyes,  
&emsp;with one chain of your neck.  
<sup>10</sup> How beautiful is your love, my sister, my bride!  
&emsp;How much better is your love than wine,  
&emsp;the fragrance of your perfumes than all kinds of spices!  
<sup>11</sup> Your lips, my bride, drip like the honeycomb.  
&emsp;Honey and milk are under your tongue.  
&emsp;The smell of your garments is like the smell of Lebanon.  
<sup>12</sup> My sister, my bride, is a locked up garden;  
&emsp;a locked up spring,  
&emsp;a sealed fountain.  
<sup>13</sup> Your shoots are an orchard of pomegranates, with precious fruits,  
&emsp;henna with spikenard plants,  
&emsp;<sup>14</sup> spikenard and saffron,  
&emsp;calamus and cinnamon, with every kind of incense tree;  
&emsp;myrrh and aloes, with all the best spices,  
&emsp;<sup>15</sup> a fountain of gardens,  
&emsp;a well of living waters,  
&emsp;flowing streams from Lebanon.

**Beloved**

<sup>16</sup> Awake, north wind, and come, you south!  
&emsp;Blow on my garden, that its spices may flow out.  
Let my beloved come into his garden,  
&emsp;and taste his precious fruits.

## Song of Solomon 5

**Lover**

<sup>1</sup> I have come into my garden, my sister, my bride.  
&emsp;I have gathered my myrrh with my spice;  
&emsp;I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey;  
&emsp;I have drunk my wine with my milk.

**Friends**

Eat, friends!  
&emsp;Drink, yes, drink abundantly, beloved.

**Beloved**

<sup>2</sup> I was asleep, but my heart was awake.  
&emsp;It is the voice of my beloved who knocks:  
&emsp;“Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled;  
&emsp;for my head is filled with dew,  
&emsp;and my hair with the dampness of the night.”  
<sup>3</sup> I have taken off my robe. Indeed, must I put it on?  
&emsp;I have washed my feet. Indeed, must I soil them?  
<sup>4</sup> My beloved thrust his hand in through the latch opening.  
&emsp;My heart pounded for him.  
<sup>5</sup> I rose up to open for my beloved.  
&emsp;My hands dripped with myrrh,  
&emsp;my fingers with liquid myrrh,  
&emsp;on the handles of the lock.  
<sup>6</sup> I opened to my beloved;  
&emsp;but my beloved left, and had gone away.  
My heart went out when he spoke.  
&emsp;I looked for him, but I didn’t find him.  
&emsp;I called him, but he didn’t answer.  
<sup>7</sup> The watchmen who go about the city found me.  
&emsp;They beat me.  
&emsp;They bruised me.  
&emsp;The keepers of the walls took my cloak away from me.

<sup>8</sup> I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem,  
&emsp;If you find my beloved,  
&emsp;that you tell him that I am faint with love.

**Friends**

<sup>9</sup> How is your beloved better than another beloved,  
&emsp;you fairest among women?  
How is your beloved better than another beloved,  
that you do so adjure us?

**Beloved**

<sup>10</sup> My beloved is white and ruddy.  
&emsp;The best among ten thousand.  
<sup>11</sup> His head is like the purest gold.  
&emsp;His hair is bushy, black as a raven.  
<sup>12</sup> His eyes are like doves beside the water brooks,  
&emsp;washed with milk, mounted like jewels.  
<sup>13</sup> His cheeks are like a bed of spices with towers of perfumes.  
&emsp;His lips are like lilies, dropping liquid myrrh.  
<sup>14</sup> His hands are like rings of gold set with beryl.  
&emsp;His body is like ivory work overlaid with sapphires.  
<sup>15</sup> His legs are like pillars of marble set on sockets of fine gold.  
&emsp;His appearance is like Lebanon, excellent as the cedars.  
<sup>16</sup> His mouth is sweetness;  
&emsp;yes, he is altogether lovely.  
This is my beloved, and this is my friend,  
&emsp;daughters of Jerusalem.

## Song of Solomon 6

**Friends**

<sup>1</sup> Where has your beloved gone, you fairest among women?  
&emsp;Where has your beloved turned, that we may seek him with you?

**Beloved**

<sup>2</sup> My beloved has gone down to his garden,  
&emsp;to the beds of spices,  
&emsp;to pasture his flock in the gardens, and to gather lilies.  
<sup>3</sup> I am my beloved’s, and my beloved is mine.  
&emsp;He browses among the lilies.

**Lover**

<sup>4</sup> You are beautiful, my love, as Tirzah,  
&emsp;lovely as Jerusalem,  
&emsp;awesome as an army with banners.  
<sup>5</sup> Turn away your eyes from me,  
&emsp;for they have overcome me.  
Your hair is like a flock of goats,  
&emsp;that lie along the side of Gilead.  
<sup>6</sup> Your teeth are like a flock of ewes,  
&emsp;which have come up from the washing,  
&emsp;of which every one has twins;  
&emsp;not one is bereaved among them.  
<sup>7</sup> Your temples are like a piece of a pomegranate behind your veil.

<sup>8</sup> There are sixty queens, eighty concubines,  
&emsp;and virgins without number.  
<sup>9</sup> My dove, my perfect one, is unique.  
&emsp;She is her mother’s only daughter.  
&emsp;She is the favorite one of her who bore her.  
The daughters saw her, and called her blessed.  
&emsp;The queens and the concubines saw her, and they praised her.

<sup>10</sup> Who is she who looks out as the morning,  
&emsp;beautiful as the moon,  
&emsp;clear as the sun,  
&emsp;and awesome as an army with banners?

<sup>11</sup> I went down into the nut tree grove,  
&emsp;to see the green plants of the valley,  
&emsp;to see whether the vine budded,  
&emsp;and the pomegranates were in flower.  
<sup>12</sup> Without realizing it,  
&emsp;my desire set me with my royal people’s chariots.

**Friends**

<sup>13</sup> Return, return, Shulammite!  
&emsp;Return, return, that we may gaze at you.

**Lover**

Why do you desire to gaze at the Shulammite,  
&emsp;as at the dance of Mahanaim?

## Song of Solomon 7

<sup>1</sup> How beautiful are your feet in sandals, prince’s daughter!

&emsp;Your rounded thighs are like jewels,  
&emsp;the work of the hands of a skillful workman.  
<sup>2</sup> Your body is like a round goblet,  
&emsp;no mixed wine is wanting.  
Your waist is like a heap of wheat,  
&emsp;set about with lilies.  
<sup>3</sup> Your two breasts are like two fawns,  
&emsp;that are twins of a roe.  
<sup>4</sup> Your neck is like an ivory tower.  
&emsp;Your eyes are like the pools in Heshbon by the gate of Bathrabbim.  
&emsp;Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon which looks toward Damascus.  
<sup>5</sup> Your head on you is like Carmel.  
&emsp;The hair of your head like purple.  
&emsp;The king is held captive in its tresses.  
<sup>6</sup> How beautiful and how pleasant you are,  
&emsp;love, for delights!  
<sup>7</sup> This, your stature, is like a palm tree,  
&emsp;your breasts like its fruit.  
<sup>8</sup> I said, “I will climb up into the palm tree.  
&emsp;I will take hold of its fruit.”  
Let your breasts be like clusters of the vine,  
&emsp;the smell of your breath like apples.  
<sup>9</sup> Your mouth is like the best wine,  
&emsp;that goes down smoothly for my beloved,  
&emsp;gliding through the lips of those who are asleep.

**Beloved**

<sup>10</sup> I am my beloved’s.  
&emsp;His desire is toward me.  
<sup>11</sup> Come, my beloved! Let’s go out into the field.  
&emsp;Let’s lodge in the villages.  
<sup>12</sup> Let’s go early up to the vineyards.  
&emsp;Let’s see whether the vine has budded,  
&emsp;its blossom is open,  
&emsp;and the pomegranates are in flower.  
&emsp;There I will give you my love.  
<sup>13</sup> The mandrakes produce fragrance.  
&emsp;At our doors are all kinds of precious fruits, new and old,  
&emsp;which I have stored up for you, my beloved.

## Song of Solomon 8

<sup>1</sup> Oh that you were like my brother,

&emsp;who nursed from the breasts of my mother!  
If I found you outside, I would kiss you;  
&emsp;yes, and no one would despise me.  
<sup>2</sup> I would lead you, bringing you into the house of my mother,  
&emsp;who would instruct me.  
I would have you drink spiced wine,  
&emsp;of the juice of my pomegranate.  
<sup>3</sup> His left hand would be under my head.  
&emsp;His right hand would embrace me.

<sup>4</sup> I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem,  
&emsp;that you not stir up, nor awaken love,  
&emsp;until it so desires.

**Friends**

<sup>5</sup> Who is this who comes up from the wilderness,  
&emsp;leaning on her beloved?

**Beloved**

Under the apple tree I awakened you.  
&emsp;There your mother conceived you.  
&emsp;There she was in labor and bore you.

<sup>6</sup> Set me as a seal on your heart,  
&emsp;as a seal on your arm;  
&emsp;for love is strong as death.  
&emsp;Jealousy is as cruel as Sheol.[^2]  
&emsp;Its flashes are flashes of fire,  
&emsp;a very flame of the LORD.[^3]  
<sup>7</sup> Many waters can’t quench love,  
&emsp;neither can floods drown it.  
If a man would give all the wealth of his house for love,  
&emsp;he would be utterly scorned.

**Brothers**

<sup>8</sup> We have a little sister.  
&emsp;She has no breasts.  
What shall we do for our sister  
&emsp;in the day when she is to be spoken for?

<sup>9</sup> If she is a wall,  
&emsp;we will build on her a turret of silver.  
If she is a door,  
&emsp;we will enclose her with boards of cedar.

**Beloved**

<sup>10</sup> I am a wall, and my breasts like towers,  
&emsp;then I was in his eyes like one who found peace.  
<sup>11</sup> Solomon had a vineyard at Baal Hamon.  
&emsp;He leased out the vineyard to keepers.  
&emsp;Each was to bring a thousand shekels[^4] of silver for its fruit.  
<sup>12</sup> My own vineyard is before me.  
&emsp;The thousand are for you, Solomon,  
&emsp;two hundred for those who tend its fruit.

**Lover**

<sup>13</sup> You who dwell in the gardens, with friends in attendance,  
&emsp;let me hear your voice!

**Beloved**

<sup>14</sup> Come away, my beloved!  
&emsp;Be like a gazelle or a young stag on the mountains of spices!

[^1]: **1:15** “Behold”, from “הִנֵּה”, means look at, take notice, observe, see, or gaze at. It is often used as an interjection.
[^2]: **8:6** Sheol is the place of the dead.
[^3]: **8:6** Hebrew: יה Yah
[^4]: **8:11** A shekel is about 10 grams or about 0.35 ounces, so 1000 shekels is about 10 kilograms or about 22 pounds.
