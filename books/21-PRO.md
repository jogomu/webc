# The Proverbs

## Proverbs 1

<sup>1</sup> The proverbs of Solomon, the son of David, king of Israel:

<sup>2</sup> to know wisdom and instruction;  
&emsp;to discern the words of understanding;  
<sup>3</sup> to receive instruction in wise dealing,  
&emsp;in righteousness, justice, and equity;  
<sup>4</sup> to give prudence to the simple,  
&emsp;knowledge and discretion to the young man—  
<sup>5</sup> that the wise man may hear, and increase in learning;  
&emsp;that the man of understanding may attain to sound counsel;  
<sup>6</sup> to understand a proverb and parables,  
&emsp;the words and riddles of the wise.

<sup>7</sup> The fear of the LORD[^1] is the beginning of knowledge,  
&emsp;but the foolish despise wisdom and instruction.  
<sup>8</sup> My son, listen to your father’s instruction,  
&emsp;and don’t forsake your mother’s teaching;  
<sup>9</sup> for they will be a ~~garland~~ <ins>diadem</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c1"></a>[c1](#c1)</sup> to grace your head,  
&emsp;and chains around your neck.  
<sup>10</sup> My son, if sinners entice you,  
&emsp;don’t consent.  
<sup>11</sup> If they say, “Come with us.  
&emsp;Let’s lie in wait for blood.  
&emsp;Let’s ~~lurk secretly for~~ <ins>hide snares against</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c2"></a>[c2](#c2)</sup> the innocent without cause.  
<sup>12</sup> Let’s swallow them up alive like Sheol,[^2]  
&emsp;and whole, like those who go down into the pit.  
<sup>13</sup> We’ll find all valuable wealth.  
&emsp;We’ll fill our houses with plunder.  
<sup>14</sup> You shall cast your lot among us.  
&emsp;We’ll all have one purse”—  
<sup>15</sup> my son, don’t walk on the path with them.  
&emsp;Keep your foot from their path,  
<sup>16</sup> for their feet run to evil.  
&emsp;They hurry to shed blood.  
<sup>17</sup> For the net is spread in vain in the sight of any bird;  
<sup>18</sup> but these lay in wait for their own blood.  
&emsp;They lurk secretly for their own lives.  
<sup>19</sup> So are the ways of everyone who is greedy for gain.  
&emsp;It takes away the life of its owners.

<sup>20</sup> Wisdom calls aloud in the street.  
&emsp;She utters her voice in the public squares.  
<sup>21</sup> She calls at the head of noisy places.  
&emsp;At the entrance of the city gates, she utters her words:  
<sup>22</sup> “How long, you simple ones, will you love simplicity?  
&emsp;How long will mockers delight themselves in mockery,  
&emsp;and fools hate knowledge?  
<sup>23</sup> Turn at my reproof.  
&emsp;Behold,[^3] I will pour out my spirit on you.  
&emsp;I will make known my words to you.  
<sup>24</sup> Because I have called, and you have refused;  
&emsp;I have stretched out my hand, and no one has paid attention;  
<sup>25</sup> but you have ignored all my counsel,  
&emsp;and wanted none of my reproof;  
<sup>26</sup> I also will laugh at your disaster.  
&emsp;I will mock when calamity overtakes you,  
<sup>27</sup> when calamity overtakes you like a storm,  
&emsp;when your disaster comes on like a whirlwind,  
&emsp;when distress and anguish come on you.  
<sup>28</sup> Then they will call on me, but I will not answer.  
&emsp;They will seek me diligently, but they will not find me,  
<sup>29</sup> because they hated ~~knowledge~~ <ins>discipline</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c3"></a>[c3](#c3)</sup>,  
&emsp;and didn’t choose the fear of the LORD.  
<sup>30</sup> They wanted none of my counsel.  
&emsp;They despised all my reproof.  
<sup>31</sup> Therefore they will eat of the fruit of their own way,  
&emsp;and be filled with their own schemes.  
<sup>32</sup> For the backsliding of the simple will kill them.  
&emsp;The careless ease of fools will destroy them.  
<sup>33</sup> But whoever listens to me will dwell securely,  
&emsp;and will be at ease, without fear of harm.”

## Proverbs 2

<sup>1</sup> My son, if you will receive my words,  
&emsp;and store up my commandments within you,  
<sup>2</sup> so as to turn your ear to wisdom,  
&emsp;and apply your heart to understanding;  
<sup>3</sup> yes, if you call out for discernment,  
&emsp;and lift up your voice for understanding;  
<sup>4</sup> if you seek her as silver,  
&emsp;and search for her as for hidden treasures;  
<sup>5</sup> then you will understand the fear of the LORD,  
&emsp;and find the knowledge of God.[^4]  
<sup>6</sup> For the LORD gives wisdom.  
&emsp;Out of his mouth comes knowledge and understanding.  
<sup>7</sup> He lays up sound wisdom for the upright.  
&emsp;He is a shield to those who walk in integrity,  
<sup>8</sup> that he may guard the paths of justice,  
&emsp;and preserve the way of his saints.  
<sup>9</sup> Then you will understand righteousness and justice,  
&emsp;equity and every good path.  
<sup>10</sup> For wisdom will enter into your heart.  
&emsp;Knowledge will be pleasant to your soul.  
<sup>11</sup> Discretion will watch over you.  
&emsp;Understanding will keep you,  
<sup>12</sup> to deliver you from the way of evil,  
&emsp;from the men who speak perverse things,  
<sup>13</sup> who forsake the paths of uprightness,  
&emsp;to walk in the ways of darkness,  
<sup>14</sup> who rejoice to do evil,  
&emsp;and delight in the perverseness of evil,  
<sup>15</sup> who are crooked in their ways,  
&emsp;and wayward in their paths,  
<sup>16</sup> to deliver you from the strange woman,  
&emsp;even from the foreigner who flatters with her words,  
<sup>17</sup> who forsakes ~~the friend of her youth~~ <ins>the guide of her youth</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c4"></a>[c4](#c4)</sup>,  
&emsp;and forgets the covenant of her God;  
<sup>18</sup> for her house leads down to death,  
&emsp;her paths to the departed spirits.  
<sup>19</sup> None who go to her return again,  
&emsp;neither do they attain to the paths of life.  
<sup>20</sup> Therefore walk in the way of good men,  
&emsp;and keep the paths of the righteous.  
<sup>21</sup> For the upright will dwell in the land.  
&emsp;The perfect will remain in it.  
<sup>22</sup> But the wicked will be cut off from the land.  
&emsp;The treacherous will be rooted out of it.

## Proverbs 3

<sup>1</sup> My son, don’t forget my teaching,  
&emsp;but let your heart keep my commandments,  
<sup>2</sup> for they will add to you length of days,  
&emsp;years of life, and peace.  
<sup>3</sup> Don’t let kindness and truth forsake you.  
&emsp;Bind them around your neck.  
&emsp;Write them on the tablet of your heart.  
<sup>4</sup> So you will find favor,  
&emsp;and good understanding in the sight of God and man.  
<sup>5</sup> Trust in the LORD with all your heart,  
&emsp;and don’t lean on your own understanding.  
<sup>6</sup> In all your ways acknowledge him,  
&emsp;and he will make your paths straight.  
<sup>7</sup> Don’t be wise in your own eyes.  
&emsp;Fear the LORD, and depart from evil.  
<sup>8</sup> It will be health to your body,  
&emsp;and nourishment to your bones.  
<sup>9</sup> Honor the LORD with your substance,  
&emsp;with the first fruits of all your increase;  
<sup>10</sup> so your barns will be filled with plenty,  
&emsp;and your vats will overflow with new wine.  
<sup>11</sup> My son, don’t despise the LORD’s discipline,  
&emsp;neither be weary of his correction;  
<sup>12</sup> for whom the LORD loves, he corrects,  
&emsp;even as a father reproves the son in whom he delights.

<sup>13</sup> Happy is the man who finds wisdom,  
&emsp;the man who gets understanding.  
<sup>14</sup> For her good profit is better than getting silver,  
&emsp;and her return is better than fine gold.  
<sup>15</sup> She is more precious than rubies.  
&emsp;None of the things you can desire are to be compared to her.  
<sup>16</sup> Length of days is in her right hand.  
&emsp;In her left hand are riches and honor.  
<sup>17</sup> Her ways are ways of pleasantness.  
&emsp;All her paths are peace.  
<sup>18</sup> She is a tree of life to those who lay hold of her.  
&emsp;Happy is everyone who retains her.  
<sup>19</sup> By wisdom the LORD founded the earth.  
&emsp;By understanding, he established the heavens.  
<sup>20</sup> By his knowledge, the depths were broken up,  
&emsp;and the skies drop down the dew.  
<sup>21</sup> My son, let them not depart from your eyes.  
&emsp;Keep sound wisdom and discretion,  
<sup>22</sup> so they will be life to your soul,  
&emsp;and grace for your neck.  
<sup>23</sup> Then you shall walk in your way securely.  
&emsp;Your foot won’t stumble.  
<sup>24</sup> When you lie down, you will not be afraid.  
&emsp;Yes, you will lie down, and your sleep will be sweet.  
<sup>25</sup> Don’t be afraid of sudden fear,  
&emsp;neither of the desolation of the wicked, when it comes;  
<sup>26</sup> for the LORD will be ~~your confidence~~ <ins>at your side</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c5"></a>[c5](#c5)</sup>,  
&emsp;and will keep your foot from being taken.

<sup>27</sup> Don’t withhold good from those to whom it is due,  
&emsp;when it is in the power of your hand to do it.  
<sup>28</sup> Don’t say to your neighbor, “Go, and come again;  
&emsp;tomorrow I will give it to you,”  
&emsp;when you have it by you.  
<sup>29</sup> Don’t devise evil against your neighbor,  
&emsp;since he dwells securely by you.  
<sup>30</sup> Don’t strive with a man without cause,  
&emsp;if he has done you no harm.  
<sup>31</sup> Don’t envy the man of violence.  
&emsp;Choose none of his ways.  
<sup>32</sup> For the perverse is an abomination to the LORD,  
&emsp;but his friendship is with the upright.  
<sup>33</sup> The LORD’s curse is in the house of the wicked,  
&emsp;but he blesses the habitation of the righteous.  
<sup>34</sup> Surely he mocks the mockers,  
&emsp;but he gives grace to the humble.  
<sup>35</sup> The wise will inherit glory,  
&emsp;but shame will be the promotion of fools.

## Proverbs 4

<sup>1</sup> Listen, sons, to a father’s instruction.  
&emsp;Pay attention and know understanding;  
<sup>2</sup> for I give you sound learning.  
&emsp;Don’t forsake my law.  
<sup>3</sup> For I was a son to my father,  
&emsp;tender and an only child in the sight of my mother.  
<sup>4</sup> He taught me, and said to me:  
&emsp;“Let your heart retain my words.  
&emsp;Keep my commandments, and live.  
<sup>5</sup> Get wisdom.  
&emsp;Get understanding.  
&emsp;Don’t forget, and don’t deviate from the words of my mouth.  
<sup>6</sup> Don’t forsake her, and she will preserve you.  
&emsp;Love her, and she will keep you.  
<sup>7</sup> Wisdom is supreme.  
&emsp;Get wisdom.  
&emsp;Yes, though it costs all your possessions, get understanding.  
<sup>8</sup> Esteem her, and she will exalt you.  
&emsp;She will bring you to honor when you embrace her.  
<sup>9</sup> She will give to your head a garland of grace.  
&emsp;She will deliver a crown of splendor to you.”

<sup>10</sup> Listen, my son, and receive my sayings.  
&emsp;The years of your life will be many.  
<sup>11</sup> I have taught you in the way of wisdom.  
&emsp;I have led you in straight paths.  
<sup>12</sup> When you go, your steps will not be hampered.  
&emsp;When you run, you will not stumble.  
<sup>13</sup> Take firm hold of instruction.  
&emsp;Don’t let her go.  
&emsp;Keep her, for she is your life.  
<sup>14</sup> Don’t enter into the path of the wicked.  
&emsp;Don’t walk in the way of evil men.  
<sup>15</sup> Avoid it, and don’t pass by it.  
&emsp;Turn from it, and pass on.  
<sup>16</sup> For they don’t sleep unless they do evil.  
&emsp;Their sleep is taken away, unless they make someone fall.  
<sup>17</sup> For they eat the bread of wickedness  
&emsp;and drink the wine of violence.  
<sup>18</sup> But the path of the righteous is like the dawning light  
&emsp;that shines more and more until the perfect day.  
<sup>19</sup> The way of the wicked is like darkness.  
&emsp;They don’t know what they stumble over.

<sup>20</sup> My son, attend to my words.  
&emsp;Turn your ear to my sayings.  
<sup>21</sup> Let them not depart from your eyes.  
&emsp;Keep them in the center of your heart.  
<sup>22</sup> For they are life to those who find them,  
&emsp;and health to their whole body.  
<sup>23</sup> Keep your heart with all diligence,  
&emsp;for out of it is the wellspring of life.  
<sup>24</sup> Put away from yourself a perverse mouth.  
&emsp;Put corrupt lips far from you.  
<sup>25</sup> Let your eyes look straight ahead.  
&emsp;Fix your gaze directly before you.  
<sup>26</sup> Make the path of your feet level.  
&emsp;Let all of your ways be established.  
<sup>27</sup> Don’t turn to the right hand nor to the left.  
&emsp;Remove your foot from evil.

## Proverbs 5

<sup>1</sup> My son, pay attention to my wisdom.  
&emsp;Turn your ear to my understanding,  
<sup>2</sup> that you may maintain discretion,  
&emsp;that your lips may preserve knowledge.  
<sup>3</sup> For the lips of an adulteress drip honey.  
&emsp;Her mouth is smoother than oil,  
<sup>4</sup> but in the end she is as bitter as wormwood,  
&emsp;and as sharp as a two-edged sword.  
<sup>5</sup> Her feet go down to death.  
&emsp;Her steps lead straight to Sheol.[^5]  
<sup>6</sup> She gives no thought to the way of life.  
&emsp;Her ways are crooked, and she doesn’t know it.

<sup>7</sup> Now therefore, my sons, listen to me.  
&emsp;Don’t depart from the words of my mouth.  
<sup>8</sup> Remove your way far from her.  
&emsp;Don’t come near the door of her house,  
<sup>9</sup> lest you give your honor to others,  
&emsp;and your years to the cruel one;  
<sup>10</sup> lest strangers feast on your wealth,  
&emsp;and your labors enrich another man’s house.  
<sup>11</sup> You will groan at your latter end,  
&emsp;when your flesh and your body are consumed,  
<sup>12</sup> and say, “How I have hated instruction,  
&emsp;and my heart despised reproof.  
<sup>13</sup> I haven’t obeyed the voice of my teachers,  
&emsp;nor turned my ear to those who instructed me!  
<sup>14</sup> I have come to the brink of utter ruin,  
&emsp;among the gathered assembly.”

<sup>15</sup> Drink water out of your own cistern,  
&emsp;running water out of your own well.  
<sup>16</sup> Should your springs overflow in the streets,  
&emsp;streams of water in the public squares?  
<sup>17</sup> Let them be for yourself alone,  
&emsp;not for strangers with you.  
<sup>18</sup> Let your spring be blessed.  
&emsp;Rejoice in the wife of your youth.  
<sup>19</sup> A loving doe and a graceful deer—  
&emsp;~~let her breasts satisfy you at all times.~~ <ins>let her caresses intoxicate you at all times.</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c6"></a>[c6](#c6)</sup>  
&emsp;Be captivated always with her love.  
<sup>20</sup> For why should you, my son, be captivated with an adulteress?  
&emsp;Why embrace the bosom of another?  
<sup>21</sup> For the ways of man are before the LORD’s eyes.  
&emsp;He examines all his paths.  
<sup>22</sup> The evil deeds of the wicked ensnare him.  
&emsp;The cords of his sin hold him firmly.  
<sup>23</sup> He will die for lack of instruction.  
&emsp;In the greatness of his folly, he will go astray.

## Proverbs 6

<sup>1</sup> My son, if you have become collateral for your neighbor,  
&emsp;if you have struck your hands in pledge for a stranger,  
<sup>2</sup> you are trapped by the words of your mouth;  
&emsp;you are ensnared with the words of your mouth.  
<sup>3</sup> Do this now, my son, and deliver yourself,  
&emsp;since you have come into the hand of your neighbor.  
Go, humble yourself.  
&emsp;Press your plea with your neighbor.  
<sup>4</sup> Give no sleep to your eyes,  
&emsp;nor slumber to your eyelids.  
<sup>5</sup> Free yourself, like a gazelle from ~~the hand of the hunter~~ <ins>the net</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c7"></a>[c7](#c7)</sup>,  
&emsp;like a bird from ~~the snare of the fowler.~~ <ins>the hand of the fowler.</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c8"></a>[c8](#c8)</sup>

<sup>6</sup> Go to the ant, you sluggard.  
&emsp;Consider her ways, and be wise;  
<sup>7</sup> which having no chief, overseer, or ruler,  
&emsp;<sup>8</sup> provides her bread in the summer,  
&emsp;and gathers her food in the harvest.  
<sup>9</sup> How long will you sleep, sluggard?  
&emsp;When will you arise out of your sleep?  
<sup>10</sup> A little sleep, a little slumber,  
&emsp;a little folding of the hands to sleep—  
<sup>11</sup> so your poverty will come as a robber,  
&emsp;and your scarcity as an armed man.

<sup>12</sup> A worthless person, a man of iniquity,  
&emsp;is he who walks with a perverse mouth,  
<sup>13</sup> who winks with his eyes, who signals with his feet,  
&emsp;who motions with his fingers,  
<sup>14</sup> in whose heart is perverseness,  
&emsp;who devises evil continually,  
&emsp;who always sows discord.  
<sup>15</sup> Therefore his calamity will come suddenly.  
&emsp;He will be broken suddenly, and that without remedy.

<sup>16</sup> There are six things which the LORD hates;  
&emsp;yes, seven which are an abomination to him:  
<sup>17</sup> arrogant eyes, a lying tongue,  
&emsp;hands that shed innocent blood,  
<sup>18</sup> a heart that devises wicked schemes,  
&emsp;feet that are swift in running to mischief,  
<sup>19</sup> a false witness who utters lies,  
&emsp;and he who sows discord among brothers.

<sup>20</sup> My son, keep your father’s commandment,  
&emsp;and don’t forsake your mother’s teaching.  
<sup>21</sup> Bind them continually on your heart.  
&emsp;Tie them around your neck.  
<sup>22</sup> When you walk, it will lead you.  
&emsp;When you sleep, it will watch over you.  
&emsp;When you awake, it will talk with you.  
<sup>23</sup> For the commandment is a lamp,  
&emsp;and the law is light.  
&emsp;Reproofs of instruction are the way of life,  
<sup>24</sup> to keep you from the immoral woman,  
&emsp;from the flattery of the wayward wife’s tongue.  
<sup>25</sup> Don’t lust after her beauty in your heart,  
&emsp;neither let her captivate you with her eyelids.  
<sup>26</sup> For a prostitute reduces you to a piece of bread.  
&emsp;The adulteress hunts for your precious life.  
<sup>27</sup> Can a man scoop fire into his lap,  
&emsp;and his clothes not be burned?  
<sup>28</sup> Or can one walk on hot coals,  
&emsp;and his feet not be scorched?  
<sup>29</sup> So is he who goes in to his neighbor’s wife.  
&emsp;Whoever touches her will not be unpunished.  
<sup>30</sup> Men don’t despise a thief  
&emsp;if he steals to satisfy himself when he is hungry,  
<sup>31</sup> but if he is found, he shall restore seven times.  
&emsp;He shall give all the wealth of his house.  
<sup>32</sup> He who commits adultery with a woman is void of understanding.  
&emsp;He who does it destroys his own soul.  
<sup>33</sup> He will get wounds and dishonor.  
&emsp;His reproach will not be wiped away.  
<sup>34</sup> For jealousy arouses the fury of the husband.  
&emsp;He won’t spare in the day of vengeance.  
<sup>35</sup> He won’t regard any ransom,  
&emsp;neither will he rest content, though you give many gifts.

## Proverbs 7

<sup>1</sup> My son, keep my words.  
&emsp;Lay up my commandments within you.  
<sup>2</sup> Keep my commandments and live!  
&emsp;Guard my teaching as the apple of your eye.  
<sup>3</sup> Bind them on your fingers.  
&emsp;Write them on the tablet of your heart.  
<sup>4</sup> Tell wisdom, “You are my sister.”  
&emsp;Call understanding your relative,  
<sup>5</sup> that they may keep you from the strange woman,  
&emsp;from the foreigner who flatters with her words.  
<sup>6</sup> For at the window of my house,  
&emsp;I looked out through my lattice.  
<sup>7</sup> I saw among the simple ones.  
&emsp;I discerned among the youths a young man void of understanding,  
<sup>8</sup> passing through the street near her corner,  
&emsp;he went the way to her house,  
<sup>9</sup> in the twilight, in the evening of the day,  
&emsp;in the middle of the night and in the darkness.  
<sup>10</sup> Behold, there a woman met him with the attire of a prostitute,  
&emsp;and with crafty intent.  
<sup>11</sup> She is loud and defiant.  
&emsp;Her feet don’t stay in her house.  
<sup>12</sup> Now she is in the streets, now in the squares,  
&emsp;and lurking at every corner.  
<sup>13</sup> So she caught him, and kissed him.  
&emsp;With an impudent face she said to him:  
<sup>14</sup> “Sacrifices of peace offerings are with me.  
&emsp;Today I have paid my vows.  
<sup>15</sup> Therefore I came out to meet you,  
&emsp;to diligently seek your face,  
&emsp;and I have found you.  
<sup>16</sup> I have spread my couch with carpets of tapestry,  
&emsp;with striped cloths of the yarn of Egypt.  
<sup>17</sup> I have perfumed my bed with myrrh, aloes, and cinnamon.  
<sup>18</sup> Come, let’s take our fill of loving until the morning.  
&emsp;Let’s solace ourselves with loving.  
<sup>19</sup> For my husband isn’t at home.  
&emsp;He has gone on a long journey.  
<sup>20</sup> He has taken a bag of money with him.  
&emsp;He will come home at the full moon.”  
<sup>21</sup> With persuasive words, she led him astray.  
&emsp;With the flattering of her lips, she seduced him.  
<sup>22</sup> ~~He followed her immediately,~~ <ins>The fool follows her,</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c10"></a>[c10](#c10)</sup>  
&emsp;~~as an ox goes to the slaughter,~~ <ins>like an ox led to the slaughter,</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c11"></a>[c11](#c11)</sup>  
&emsp;~~as a fool stepping into a noose.~~ <ins>like a stag ensnared in a bond.</ins><sup>NV</sup><sup><a name="ref-c9"></a>[c9](#c9)</sup>  
<sup>23</sup> Until an arrow strikes through his liver,  
&emsp;as a bird hurries to the snare,  
&emsp;and doesn’t know that it will cost his life.

<sup>24</sup> Now therefore, sons, listen to me.  
&emsp;Pay attention to the words of my mouth.  
<sup>25</sup> Don’t let your heart turn to her ways.  
&emsp;Don’t go astray in her paths,  
<sup>26</sup> for she has thrown down many wounded.  
&emsp;Yes, all her slain are a mighty army.  
<sup>27</sup> Her house is the way to Sheol,[^6]  
&emsp;going down to the rooms of death.

## Proverbs 8

<sup>1</sup> Doesn’t wisdom cry out?  
&emsp;Doesn’t understanding raise her voice?  
<sup>2</sup> On the top of high places by the way,  
&emsp;where the paths meet, she stands.  
<sup>3</sup> Beside the gates, at the entry of the city,  
&emsp;at the entry doors, she cries aloud:  
<sup>4</sup> “I call to you men!  
&emsp;I send my voice to the sons of mankind.  
<sup>5</sup> You simple, understand prudence!  
&emsp;You fools, be of an understanding heart!  
<sup>6</sup> Hear, for I will speak excellent things.  
&emsp;The opening of my lips is for right things.

&emsp;so that your trust may be in the LORD.  
<sup>20</sup> Haven’t I written to you thirty excellent things  
&emsp;of counsel and knowledge,  
<sup>21</sup> To teach you truth, reliable words,  
&emsp;to give sound answers to the ones who sent you?

<sup>22</sup> Don’t exploit the poor because he is poor;  
&emsp;and don’t crush the needy in court;  
<sup>23</sup> for the LORD will plead their case,  
&emsp;and plunder the life of those who plunder them.

<sup>24</sup> Don’t befriend a hot-tempered man.  
&emsp;Don’t associate with one who harbors anger,  
<sup>25</sup> lest you learn his ways  
&emsp;and ensnare your soul.

<sup>26</sup> Don’t you be one of those who strike hands,  
&emsp;of those who are collateral for debts.  
<sup>27</sup> If you don’t have means to pay,  
&emsp;why should he take away your bed from under you?

<sup>28</sup> Don’t move the ancient boundary stone  
&emsp;which your fathers have set up.

<sup>29</sup> Do you see a man skilled in his work?  
&emsp;He will serve kings.  
&emsp;He won’t serve obscure men.

## Proverbs 23

<sup>1</sup> When you sit to eat with a ruler,  
&emsp;consider diligently what is before you;  
<sup>2</sup> put a knife to your throat  
&emsp;if you are a man given to appetite.  
<sup>3</sup> Don’t be desirous of his dainties,  
&emsp;since they are deceitful food.  
<sup>4</sup> Don’t weary yourself to be rich.  
&emsp;In your wisdom, show restraint.  
<sup>5</sup> Why do you set your eyes on that which is not?  
&emsp;For it certainly sprouts wings like an eagle and flies in the sky.  
<sup>6</sup> Don’t eat the food of him who has a stingy eye,  
&emsp;and don’t crave his delicacies,  
&emsp;<sup>7</sup> for as he thinks about the cost, so he is.  
&emsp;“Eat and drink!” he says to you,  
&emsp;but his heart is not with you.  
<sup>8</sup> You will vomit up the morsel which you have eaten  
&emsp;and waste your pleasant words.

<sup>9</sup> Don’t speak in the ears of a fool,  
&emsp;for he will despise the wisdom of your words.

<sup>10</sup> Don’t move the ancient boundary stone.  
&emsp;Don’t encroach on the fields of the fatherless,  
<sup>11</sup> for their Defender is strong.  
&emsp;He will plead their case against you.

<sup>12</sup> Apply your heart to instruction,  
&emsp;and your ears to the words of knowledge.  
<sup>13</sup> Don’t withhold correction from a child.  
&emsp;If you punish him with the rod, he will not die.  
<sup>14</sup> Punish him with the rod,  
&emsp;and save his soul from Sheol.[^11]

<sup>15</sup> My son, if your heart is wise,  
&emsp;then my heart will be glad, even mine.  
<sup>16</sup> Yes, my heart will rejoice  
&emsp;when your lips speak what is right.  
<sup>17</sup> Don’t let your heart envy sinners,  
&emsp;but rather fear the LORD all day long.  
<sup>18</sup> Indeed surely there is a future hope,  
&emsp;and your hope will not be cut off.  
<sup>19</sup> Listen, my son, and be wise,  
&emsp;and keep your heart on the right path!  
<sup>20</sup> Don’t be among ones drinking too much wine,  
&emsp;or those who gorge themselves on meat;  
<sup>21</sup> for the drunkard and the glutton shall become poor;  
&emsp;and drowsiness clothes them in rags.  
<sup>22</sup> Listen to your father who gave you life,  
&emsp;and don’t despise your mother when she is old.  
<sup>23</sup> Buy the truth, and don’t sell it.  
&emsp;Get wisdom, discipline, and understanding.  
<sup>24</sup> The father of the righteous has great joy.  
&emsp;Whoever fathers a wise child delights in him.  
<sup>25</sup> Let your father and your mother be glad!  
&emsp;Let her who bore you rejoice!  
<sup>26</sup> My son, give me your heart;  
&emsp;and let your eyes keep in my ways.  
<sup>27</sup> For a prostitute is a deep pit;  
&emsp;and a wayward wife is a narrow well.  
<sup>28</sup> Yes, she lies in wait like a robber,  
&emsp;and increases the unfaithful among men.

<sup>29</sup> Who has woe?  
&emsp;Who has sorrow?  
&emsp;Who has strife?  
&emsp;Who has complaints?  
&emsp;Who has needless bruises?  
&emsp;Who has bloodshot eyes?  
<sup>30</sup> Those who stay long at the wine;  
&emsp;those who go to seek out mixed wine.  
<sup>31</sup> Don’t look at the wine when it is red,  
&emsp;when it sparkles in the cup,  
&emsp;when it goes down smoothly.  
<sup>32</sup> In the end, it bites like a snake,  
&emsp;and poisons like a viper.  
<sup>33</sup> Your eyes will see strange things,  
&emsp;and your mind will imagine confusing things.  
<sup>34</sup> Yes, you will be as he who lies down in the middle of the sea,  
&emsp;or as he who lies on top of the rigging:  
<sup>35</sup> “They hit me, and I was not hurt!  
&emsp;They beat me, and I don’t feel it!  
&emsp;When will I wake up? I can do it again.  
&emsp;I will look for more.”

## Proverbs 24

<sup>1</sup> Don’t be envious of evil men,  
&emsp;neither desire to be with them;  
<sup>2</sup> for their hearts plot violence  
&emsp;and their lips talk about mischief.  
<sup>3</sup> Through wisdom a house is built;  
&emsp;by understanding it is established;  
<sup>4</sup> by knowledge the rooms are filled  
&emsp;with all rare and beautiful treasure.  
<sup>5</sup> A wise man has great power.  
&emsp;A knowledgeable man increases strength,  
<sup>6</sup> for by wise guidance you wage your war,  
&emsp;and victory is in many advisors.  
<sup>7</sup> Wisdom is too high for a fool.  
&emsp;He doesn’t open his mouth in the gate.  
<sup>8</sup> One who plots to do evil  
&emsp;will be called a schemer.  
<sup>9</sup> The schemes of folly are sin.  
&emsp;The mocker is detested by men.  
<sup>10</sup> If you falter in the time of trouble,  
&emsp;your strength is small.  
<sup>11</sup> Rescue those who are being led away to death!  
&emsp;Indeed, hold back those who are staggering to the slaughter!  
<sup>12</sup> If you say, “Behold, we didn’t know this,”  
&emsp;doesn’t he who weighs the hearts consider it?  
He who keeps your soul, doesn’t he know it?  
&emsp;Shall he not give to every man according to his work?  
<sup>13</sup> My son, eat honey, for it is good,  
&emsp;the droppings of the honeycomb, which are sweet to your taste;  
<sup>14</sup> so you shall know wisdom to be to your soul.  
&emsp;If you have found it, then there will be a reward:  
&emsp;Your hope will not be cut off.  
<sup>15</sup> Don’t lay in wait, wicked man, against the habitation of the righteous.  
&emsp;Don’t destroy his resting place;  
<sup>16</sup> for a righteous man falls seven times and rises up again,  
&emsp;but the wicked are overthrown by calamity.  
<sup>17</sup> Don’t rejoice when your enemy falls.  
&emsp;Don’t let your heart be glad when he is overthrown,  
<sup>18</sup> lest the LORD see it, and it displease him,  
&emsp;and he turn away his wrath from him.  
<sup>19</sup> Don’t fret yourself because of evildoers,  
&emsp;neither be envious of the wicked;  
<sup>20</sup> for there will be no reward to the evil man.  
&emsp;The lamp of the wicked will be snuffed out.  
<sup>21</sup> My son, fear the LORD and the king.  
&emsp;Don’t join those who are rebellious,  
<sup>22</sup> for their calamity will rise suddenly.  
&emsp;Who knows what destruction may come from them both?

<sup>23</sup> These also are sayings of the wise:

To show partiality in judgment is not good.  
<sup>24</sup> He who says to the wicked, “You are righteous,”  
&emsp;peoples will curse him, and nations will abhor him—  
<sup>25</sup> but it will go well with those who convict the guilty,  
&emsp;and a rich blessing will come on them.  
<sup>26</sup> An honest answer  
&emsp;is like a kiss on the lips.  
<sup>27</sup> Prepare your work outside,  
&emsp;and get your fields ready.  
&emsp;Afterwards, build your house.  
<sup>28</sup> Don’t be a witness against your neighbor without cause.  
&emsp;Don’t deceive with your lips.  
<sup>29</sup> Don’t say, “I will do to him as he has done to me;  
&emsp;I will repay the man according to his work.”  
<sup>30</sup> I went by the field of the sluggard,  
&emsp;by the vineyard of the man void of understanding.  
<sup>31</sup> Behold, it was all grown over with thorns.  
&emsp;Its surface was covered with nettles,  
&emsp;and its stone wall was broken down.  
<sup>32</sup> Then I saw, and considered well.  
&emsp;I saw, and received instruction:  
<sup>33</sup> a little sleep, a little slumber,  
&emsp;a little folding of the hands to sleep,  
<sup>34</sup> so your poverty will come as a robber  
&emsp;and your want as an armed man.

## Proverbs 25

<sup>1</sup> These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out.

<sup>2</sup> It is the glory of God to conceal a thing,  
&emsp;but the glory of kings is to search out a matter.  
<sup>3</sup> As the heavens for height, and the earth for depth,  
&emsp;so the hearts of kings are unsearchable.  
<sup>4</sup> Take away the dross from the silver,  
&emsp;and material comes out for the refiner.  
<sup>5</sup> Take away the wicked from the king’s presence,  
&emsp;and his throne will be established in righteousness.  
<sup>6</sup> Don’t exalt yourself in the presence of the king,  
&emsp;or claim a place among great men;  
<sup>7</sup> for it is better that it be said to you, “Come up here,”  
&emsp;than that you should be put lower in the presence of the prince,  
&emsp;whom your eyes have seen.  
<sup>8</sup> Don’t be hasty in bringing charges to court.  
&emsp;What will you do in the end when your neighbor shames you?  
<sup>9</sup> Debate your case with your neighbor,  
&emsp;and don’t betray the confidence of another,  
&emsp;<sup>10</sup> lest one who hears it put you to shame,  
&emsp;and your bad reputation never depart.

<sup>11</sup> A word fitly spoken  
&emsp;is like apples of gold in settings of silver.  
<sup>12</sup> As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold,  
&emsp;so is a wise reprover to an obedient ear.  
<sup>13</sup> As the cold of snow in the time of harvest,  
&emsp;so is a faithful messenger to those who send him;  
&emsp;for he refreshes the soul of his masters.  
<sup>14</sup> As clouds and wind without rain,  
&emsp;so is he who boasts of gifts deceptively.  
<sup>15</sup> By patience a ruler is persuaded.  
&emsp;A soft tongue breaks the bone.  
<sup>16</sup> Have you found honey?  
&emsp;Eat as much as is sufficient for you,  
&emsp;lest you eat too much, and vomit it.  
<sup>17</sup> Let your foot be seldom in your neighbor’s house,  
&emsp;lest he be weary of you, and hate you.  
<sup>18</sup> A man who gives false testimony against his neighbor  
&emsp;is like a club, a sword, or a sharp arrow.  
<sup>19</sup> Confidence in someone unfaithful in time of trouble  
&emsp;is like a bad tooth or a lame foot.  
<sup>20</sup> As one who takes away a garment in cold weather,  
&emsp;or vinegar on soda,  
&emsp;so is one who sings songs to a heavy heart.  
<sup>21</sup> If your enemy is hungry, give him food to eat.  
&emsp;If he is thirsty, give him water to drink;  
<sup>22</sup> for you will heap coals of fire on his head,  
&emsp;and the LORD will reward you.  
<sup>23</sup> The north wind produces rain;  
&emsp;so a backbiting tongue brings an angry face.  
<sup>24</sup> It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop  
&emsp;than to share a house with a contentious woman.  
<sup>25</sup> Like cold water to a thirsty soul,  
&emsp;so is good news from a far country.  
<sup>26</sup> Like a muddied spring and a polluted well,  
&emsp;so is a righteous man who gives way before the wicked.  
<sup>27</sup> It is not good to eat much honey,  
&emsp;nor is it honorable to seek one’s own honor.  
<sup>28</sup> Like a city that is broken down and without walls  
&emsp;is a man whose spirit is without restraint.

## Proverbs 26

<sup>1</sup> Like snow in summer, and as rain in harvest,  
&emsp;so honor is not fitting for a fool.  
<sup>2</sup> Like a fluttering sparrow,  
&emsp;like a darting swallow,  
&emsp;so the undeserved curse doesn’t come to rest.  
<sup>3</sup> A whip is for the horse,  
&emsp;a bridle for the donkey,  
&emsp;and a rod for the back of fools!  
<sup>4</sup> Don’t answer a fool according to his folly,  
&emsp;lest you also be like him.  
<sup>5</sup> Answer a fool according to his folly,  
&emsp;lest he be wise in his own eyes.  
<sup>6</sup> One who sends a message by the hand of a fool  
&emsp;is cutting off feet and drinking violence.  
<sup>7</sup> Like the legs of the lame that hang loose,  
&emsp;so is a parable in the mouth of fools.  
<sup>8</sup> As one who binds a stone in a sling,  
&emsp;so is he who gives honor to a fool.  
<sup>9</sup> Like a thorn bush that goes into the hand of a drunkard,  
&emsp;so is a parable in the mouth of fools.  
<sup>10</sup> As an archer who wounds all,  
&emsp;so is he who hires a fool  
&emsp;or he who hires those who pass by.  
<sup>11</sup> As a dog that returns to his vomit,  
&emsp;so is a fool who repeats his folly.  
<sup>12</sup> Do you see a man wise in his own eyes?  
&emsp;There is more hope for a fool than for him.  
<sup>13</sup> The sluggard says, “There is a lion in the road!  
&emsp;A fierce lion roams the streets!”  
<sup>14</sup> As the door turns on its hinges,  
&emsp;so does the sluggard on his bed.  
<sup>15</sup> The sluggard buries his hand in the dish.  
&emsp;He is too lazy to bring it back to his mouth.  
<sup>16</sup> The sluggard is wiser in his own eyes  
&emsp;than seven men who answer with discretion.  
<sup>17</sup> Like one who grabs a dog’s ears  
&emsp;is one who passes by and meddles in a quarrel not his own.  
<sup>18</sup> Like a madman who shoots torches, arrows, and death,  
&emsp;<sup>19</sup> is the man who deceives his neighbor and says, “Am I not joking?”  
<sup>20</sup> For lack of wood a fire goes out.  
&emsp;Without gossip, a quarrel dies down.  
<sup>21</sup> As coals are to hot embers,  
&emsp;and wood to fire,  
&emsp;so is a contentious man to kindling strife.  
<sup>22</sup> The words of a whisperer are as dainty morsels,  
&emsp;they go down into the innermost parts.  
<sup>23</sup> Like silver dross on an earthen vessel  
&emsp;are the lips of a fervent one with an evil heart.  
<sup>24</sup> A malicious man disguises himself with his lips,  
&emsp;but he harbors evil in his heart.  
<sup>25</sup> When his speech is charming, don’t believe him,  
&emsp;for there are seven abominations in his heart.  
<sup>26</sup> His malice may be concealed by deception,  
&emsp;but his wickedness will be exposed in the assembly.  
<sup>27</sup> Whoever digs a pit shall fall into it.  
&emsp;Whoever rolls a stone, it will come back on him.  
<sup>28</sup> A lying tongue hates those it hurts;  
&emsp;and a flattering mouth works ruin.

## Proverbs 27

<sup>1</sup> Don’t boast about tomorrow;  
&emsp;for you don’t know what a day may bring.  
<sup>2</sup> Let another man praise you,  
&emsp;and not your own mouth;  
&emsp;a stranger, and not your own lips.  
<sup>3</sup> A stone is heavy,  
&emsp;and sand is a burden;  
&emsp;but a fool’s provocation is heavier than both.  
<sup>4</sup> Wrath is cruel,  
&emsp;and anger is overwhelming;  
&emsp;but who is able to stand before jealousy?  
<sup>5</sup> Better is open rebuke  
&emsp;than hidden love.  
<sup>6</sup> The wounds of a friend are faithful,  
&emsp;although the kisses of an enemy are profuse.  
<sup>7</sup> A full soul loathes a honeycomb;  
&emsp;but to a hungry soul, every bitter thing is sweet.  
<sup>8</sup> As a bird that wanders from her nest,  
&emsp;so is a man who wanders from his home.  
<sup>9</sup> Perfume and incense bring joy to the heart;  
&emsp;so does earnest counsel from a man’s friend.  
<sup>10</sup> Don’t forsake your friend and your father’s friend.  
&emsp;Don’t go to your brother’s house in the day of your disaster.  
&emsp;A neighbor who is near is better than a distant brother.  
<sup>11</sup> Be wise, my son,  
&emsp;and bring joy to my heart,  
&emsp;then I can answer my tormentor.  
<sup>12</sup> A prudent man sees danger and takes refuge;  
&emsp;but the simple pass on, and suffer for it.  
<sup>13</sup> Take his garment when he puts up collateral for a stranger.  
&emsp;Hold it for a wayward woman!  
<sup>14</sup> He who blesses his neighbor with a loud voice early in the morning,  
&emsp;it will be taken as a curse by him.  
<sup>15</sup> A continual dropping on a rainy day  
&emsp;and a contentious wife are alike:  
<sup>16</sup> restraining her is like restraining the wind,  
&emsp;or like grasping oil in his right hand.

<sup>17</sup> Iron sharpens iron;  
&emsp;so a man sharpens his friend’s countenance.  
<sup>18</sup> Whoever tends the fig tree shall eat its fruit.  
&emsp;He who looks after his master shall be honored.  
<sup>19</sup> Like water reflects a face,  
&emsp;so a man’s heart reflects the man.  
<sup>20</sup> Sheol[^12] and Abaddon are never satisfied;  
&emsp;and a man’s eyes are never satisfied.  
<sup>21</sup> The crucible is for silver,  
&emsp;and the furnace for gold;  
&emsp;but man is refined by his praise.  
<sup>22</sup> Though you grind a fool in a mortar with a pestle along with grain,  
&emsp;yet his foolishness will not be removed from him.

<sup>23</sup> Know well the state of your flocks,  
&emsp;and pay attention to your herds,  
<sup>24</sup> for riches are not forever,  
&emsp;nor does the crown endure to all generations.  
<sup>25</sup> The hay is removed, and the new growth appears,  
&emsp;the grasses of the hills are gathered in.  
<sup>26</sup> The lambs are for your clothing,  
&emsp;and the goats are the price of a field.  
<sup>27</sup> There will be plenty of goats’ milk for your food,  
&emsp;for your family’s food,  
&emsp;and for the nourishment of your servant girls.

## Proverbs 28

<sup>1</sup> The wicked flee when no one pursues;  
&emsp;but the righteous are as bold as a lion.  
<sup>2</sup> In rebellion, a land has many rulers,  
&emsp;but order is maintained by a man of understanding and knowledge.  
<sup>3</sup> A needy man who oppresses the poor  
&emsp;is like a driving rain which leaves no crops.  
<sup>4</sup> Those who forsake the law praise the wicked;  
&emsp;but those who keep the law contend with them.  
<sup>5</sup> Evil men don’t understand justice;  
&emsp;but those who seek the LORD understand it fully.  
<sup>6</sup> Better is the poor who walks in his integrity  
&emsp;than he who is perverse in his ways, and he is rich.  
<sup>7</sup> Whoever keeps the law is a wise son;  
&emsp;but he who is a companion of gluttons shames his father.  
<sup>8</sup> He who increases his wealth by excessive interest  
&emsp;gathers it for one who has pity on the poor.  
<sup>9</sup> He who turns away his ear from hearing the law,  
&emsp;even his prayer is an abomination.  
<sup>10</sup> Whoever causes the upright to go astray in an evil way,  
&emsp;he will fall into his own trap;  
&emsp;but the blameless will inherit good.  
<sup>11</sup> The rich man is wise in his own eyes;  
&emsp;but the poor who has understanding sees through him.  
<sup>12</sup> When the righteous triumph, there is great glory;  
&emsp;but when the wicked rise, men hide themselves.  
<sup>13</sup> He who conceals his sins doesn’t prosper,  
&emsp;but whoever confesses and renounces them finds mercy.  
<sup>14</sup> Blessed is the man who always fears;  
&emsp;but one who hardens his heart falls into trouble.  
<sup>15</sup> As a roaring lion or a charging bear,  
&emsp;so is a wicked ruler over helpless people.  
<sup>16</sup> A tyrannical ruler lacks judgment.  
&emsp;One who hates ill-gotten gain will have long days.  
<sup>17</sup> A man who is tormented by blood guilt will be a fugitive until death.  
&emsp;No one will support him.  
<sup>18</sup> Whoever walks blamelessly is kept safe;  
&emsp;but one with perverse ways will fall suddenly.  
<sup>19</sup> One who works his land will have an abundance of food;  
&emsp;but one who chases fantasies will have his fill of poverty.  
<sup>20</sup> A faithful man is rich with blessings;  
&emsp;but one who is eager to be rich will not go unpunished.  
<sup>21</sup> To show partiality is not good,  
&emsp;yet a man will do wrong for a piece of bread.  
<sup>22</sup> A stingy man hurries after riches,  
&emsp;and doesn’t know that poverty waits for him.  
<sup>23</sup> One who rebukes a man will afterward find more favor  
&emsp;than one who flatters with the tongue.  
<sup>24</sup> Whoever robs his father or his mother and says, “It’s not wrong,”  
&emsp;is a partner with a destroyer.  
<sup>25</sup> One who is greedy stirs up strife;  
&emsp;but one who trusts in the LORD will prosper.  
<sup>26</sup> One who trusts in himself is a fool;  
&emsp;but one who walks in wisdom is kept safe.  
<sup>27</sup> One who gives to the poor has no lack;  
&emsp;but one who closes his eyes will have many curses.  
<sup>28</sup> When the wicked rise, men hide themselves;  
&emsp;but when they perish, the righteous thrive.

## Proverbs 29

<sup>1</sup> He who is often rebuked and stiffens his neck  
&emsp;will be destroyed suddenly, with no remedy.  
<sup>2</sup> When the righteous thrive, the people rejoice;  
&emsp;but when the wicked rule, the people groan.  
<sup>3</sup> Whoever loves wisdom brings joy to his father;  
&emsp;but a companion of prostitutes squanders his wealth.  
<sup>4</sup> The king by justice makes the land stable,  
&emsp;but he who takes bribes tears it down.  
<sup>5</sup> A man who flatters his neighbor  
&emsp;spreads a net for his feet.  
<sup>6</sup> An evil man is snared by his sin,  
&emsp;but the righteous can sing and be glad.  
<sup>7</sup> The righteous care about justice for the poor.  
&emsp;The wicked aren’t concerned about knowledge.  
<sup>8</sup> Mockers stir up a city,  
&emsp;but wise men turn away anger.  
<sup>9</sup> If a wise man goes to court with a foolish man,  
&emsp;the fool rages or scoffs, and there is no peace.  
<sup>10</sup> The bloodthirsty hate a man of integrity;  
&emsp;and they seek the life of the upright.  
<sup>11</sup> A fool vents all of his anger,  
&emsp;but a wise man brings himself under control.  
<sup>12</sup> If a ruler listens to lies,  
&emsp;all of his officials are wicked.  
<sup>13</sup> The poor man and the oppressor have this in common:  
&emsp;The LORD gives sight to the eyes of both.  
<sup>14</sup> The king who fairly judges the poor,  
&emsp;his throne shall be established forever.  
<sup>15</sup> The rod of correction gives wisdom,  
&emsp;but a child left to himself causes shame to his mother.  
<sup>16</sup> When the wicked increase, sin increases;  
&emsp;but the righteous will see their downfall.  
<sup>17</sup> Correct your son, and he will give you peace;  
&emsp;yes, he will bring delight to your soul.  
<sup>18</sup> Where there is no revelation, the people cast off restraint;  
&emsp;but one who keeps the law is blessed.  
<sup>19</sup> A servant can’t be corrected by words.  
&emsp;Though he understands, yet he will not respond.  
<sup>20</sup> Do you see a man who is hasty in his words?  
&emsp;There is more hope for a fool than for him.  
<sup>21</sup> He who pampers his servant from youth  
&emsp;will have him become a son in the end.  
<sup>22</sup> An angry man stirs up strife,  
&emsp;and a wrathful man abounds in sin.  
<sup>23</sup> A man’s pride brings him low,  
&emsp;but one of lowly spirit gains honor.  
<sup>24</sup> Whoever is an accomplice of a thief is an enemy of his own soul.  
&emsp;He takes an oath, but dares not testify.  
<sup>25</sup> The fear of man proves to be a snare,  
&emsp;but whoever puts his trust in the LORD is kept safe.  
<sup>26</sup> Many seek the ruler’s favor,  
&emsp;but a man’s justice comes from the LORD.  
<sup>27</sup> A dishonest man detests the righteous,  
&emsp;and the upright in their ways detest the wicked.

## Proverbs 30

<sup>1</sup> The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, the revelation:

the man says to Ithiel,  
&emsp;to Ithiel and Ucal:  
<sup>2</sup> “Surely I am the most ignorant man,  
&emsp;and don’t have a man’s understanding.  
<sup>3</sup> I have not learned wisdom,  
&emsp;neither do I have the knowledge of the Holy One.  
<sup>4</sup> Who has ascended up into heaven, and descended?  
&emsp;Who has gathered the wind in his fists?  
&emsp;Who has bound the waters in his garment?  
&emsp;Who has established all the ends of the earth?  
&emsp;What is his name, and what is his son’s name, if you know?

<sup>5</sup> “Every word of God is flawless.  
&emsp;He is a shield to those who take refuge in him.  
<sup>6</sup> Don’t you add to his words,  
&emsp;lest he reprove you, and you be found a liar.

<sup>7</sup> “Two things I have asked of you.  
&emsp;Don’t deny me before I die.  
<sup>8</sup> Remove far from me falsehood and lies.  
&emsp;Give me neither poverty nor riches.  
&emsp;Feed me with the food that is needful for me,  
<sup>9</sup> lest I be full, deny you, and say, ‘Who is the LORD?’  
&emsp;or lest I be poor, and steal,  
&emsp;and so dishonor the name of my God.

<sup>10</sup> “Don’t slander a servant to his master,  
&emsp;lest he curse you, and you be held guilty.

<sup>11</sup> There is a generation that curses their father,  
&emsp;and doesn’t bless their mother.  
<sup>12</sup> There is a generation that is pure in their own eyes,  
&emsp;yet are not washed from their filthiness.  
<sup>13</sup> There is a generation, oh how lofty are their eyes!  
&emsp;Their eyelids are lifted up.  
<sup>14</sup> There is a generation whose teeth are like swords,  
&emsp;and their jaws like knives,  
&emsp;to devour the poor from the earth, and the needy from among men.

<sup>15</sup> “The leech has two daughters:  
&emsp;‘Give, give.’

“There are three things that are never satisfied;  
&emsp;four that don’t say, ‘Enough!’:  
&emsp;<sup>16</sup> Sheol,[^13]  
&emsp;the barren womb,  
&emsp;the earth that is not satisfied with water,  
&emsp;and the fire that doesn’t say, ‘Enough!’

<sup>17</sup> “The eye that mocks at his father,  
&emsp;and scorns obedience to his mother,  
&emsp;the ravens of the valley shall pick it out,  
&emsp;the young eagles shall eat it.

<sup>18</sup> “There are three things which are too amazing for me,  
&emsp;four which I don’t understand:  
&emsp;<sup>19</sup> The way of an eagle in the air,  
&emsp;the way of a serpent on a rock,  
&emsp;the way of a ship in the middle of the sea,  
&emsp;and the way of a man with a maiden.

<sup>20</sup> “So is the way of an adulterous woman:  
&emsp;She eats and wipes her mouth,  
&emsp;and says, ‘I have done nothing wrong.’

<sup>21</sup> “For three things the earth trembles,  
&emsp;and under four, it can’t bear up:  
&emsp;<sup>22</sup> For a servant when he is king,  
&emsp;a fool when he is filled with food,  
&emsp;<sup>23</sup> for an unloved woman when she is married,  
&emsp;and a servant who is heir to her mistress.

<sup>24</sup> “There are four things which are little on the earth,  
&emsp;but they are exceedingly wise:  
&emsp;<sup>25</sup> The ants are not a strong people,  
&emsp;yet they provide their food in the summer.  
&emsp;<sup>26</sup> The hyraxes are but a feeble folk,  
&emsp;yet make they their houses in the rocks.  
&emsp;<sup>27</sup> The locusts have no king,  
&emsp;yet they advance in ranks.  
&emsp;<sup>28</sup> You can catch a lizard with your hands,  
&emsp;yet it is in kings’ palaces.

<sup>29</sup> “There are three things which are stately in their march,  
&emsp;four which are stately in going:  
&emsp;<sup>30</sup> The lion, which is mightiest among animals,  
&emsp;and doesn’t turn away for any;  
&emsp;<sup>31</sup> the greyhound;  
&emsp;the male goat;  
&emsp;and the king against whom there is no rising up.

<sup>32</sup> “If you have done foolishly in lifting up yourself,  
&emsp;or if you have thought evil,  
put your hand over your mouth.  
&emsp;<sup>33</sup> For as the churning of milk produces butter,  
&emsp;and the wringing of the nose produces blood,  
&emsp;so the forcing of wrath produces strife.”

## Proverbs 31

<sup>1</sup> The words of King Lemuel—the revelation which his mother taught him:

<sup>2</sup> “Oh, my son!  
&emsp;Oh, son of my womb!  
&emsp;Oh, son of my vows!  
<sup>3</sup> Don’t give your strength to women,  
&emsp;nor your ways to that which destroys kings.  
<sup>4</sup> It is not for kings, Lemuel,  
&emsp;it is not for kings to drink wine,  
&emsp;nor for princes to say, ‘Where is strong drink?’  
<sup>5</sup> lest they drink, and forget the law,  
&emsp;and pervert the justice due to anyone who is afflicted.  
<sup>6</sup> Give strong drink to him who is ready to perish,  
&emsp;and wine to the bitter in soul.  
<sup>7</sup> Let him drink, and forget his poverty,  
&emsp;and remember his misery no more.  
<sup>8</sup> Open your mouth for the mute,  
&emsp;in the cause of all who are left desolate.  
<sup>9</sup> Open your mouth, judge righteously,  
&emsp;and serve justice to the poor and needy.”

<sup>10</sup> [^14]Who can find a worthy woman?  
&emsp;For her value is far above rubies.  
<sup>11</sup> The heart of her husband trusts in her.  
&emsp;He shall have no lack of gain.  
<sup>12</sup> She does him good, and not harm,  
&emsp;all the days of her life.  
<sup>13</sup> She seeks wool and flax,  
&emsp;and works eagerly with her hands.  
<sup>14</sup> She is like the merchant ships.  
&emsp;She brings her bread from afar.  
<sup>15</sup> She rises also while it is yet night,  
&emsp;gives food to her household,  
&emsp;and portions for her servant girls.  
<sup>16</sup> She considers a field, and buys it.  
&emsp;With the fruit of her hands, she plants a vineyard.  
<sup>17</sup> She arms her waist with strength,  
&emsp;and makes her arms strong.  
<sup>18</sup> She perceives that her merchandise is profitable.  
&emsp;Her lamp doesn’t go out by night.  
<sup>19</sup> She lays her hands to the distaff,  
&emsp;and her hands hold the spindle.  
<sup>20</sup> She opens her arms to the poor;  
&emsp;yes, she extends her hands to the needy.  
<sup>21</sup> She is not afraid of the snow for her household,  
&emsp;for all her household are clothed with scarlet.  
<sup>22</sup> She makes for herself carpets of tapestry.  
&emsp;Her clothing is fine linen and purple.  
<sup>23</sup> Her husband is respected in the gates,  
&emsp;when he sits among the elders of the land.  
<sup>24</sup> She makes linen garments and sells them,  
&emsp;and delivers sashes to the merchant.  
<sup>25</sup> Strength and dignity are her clothing.  
&emsp;She laughs at the time to come.  
<sup>26</sup> She opens her mouth with wisdom.  
&emsp;Kind instruction is on her tongue.  
<sup>27</sup> She looks well to the ways of her household,  
&emsp;and doesn’t eat the bread of idleness.  
<sup>28</sup> Her children rise up and call her blessed.  
&emsp;Her husband also praises her:  
<sup>29</sup> “Many women do noble things,  
&emsp;but you excel them all.”  
<sup>30</sup> Charm is deceitful, and beauty is vain;  
&emsp;but a woman who fears the LORD, she shall be praised.  
<sup>31</sup> Give her of the fruit of her hands!  
&emsp;Let her works praise her in the gates!
