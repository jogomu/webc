# The Wisdom of Solomon

*[World English Bible (Catholic)](README.md) — Text version: signed by eBible.org 2026-07-24 (pulled 2026-07-26).*

The *Wisdom of Solomon* is recognized as Deuterocanonical Scripture by the Roman Catholic, Greek Orthodox, and Russian Orthodox Churches.

## Wisdom of Solomon 1

<sup>1</sup> Love righteousness, all you who are judges of the earth.  
&emsp;Think of the Lord[^1] with a good mind.  
Seek him in singleness of heart,  
&emsp;<sup>2</sup> because he is found by those who don’t put him to the test,  
&emsp;and is manifested to those who trust him.  
<sup>3</sup> for crooked thoughts separate from God.  
&emsp;His Power convicts when it is tested,  
&emsp;and exposes the foolish;  
<sup>4</sup> because wisdom will not enter into a soul that devises evil,  
&emsp;nor dwell in a body that is enslaved by sin.  
<sup>5</sup> For a holy spirit of discipline will flee deceit,  
&emsp;and will depart from thoughts that are without understanding,  
&emsp;and will be ashamed when unrighteousness has come in.

<sup>6</sup> For[^2] wisdom is a spirit who loves man,  
&emsp;and she will not hold a[^3] blasphemer guiltless for his lips,  
&emsp;because God is witness of his inmost self,  
&emsp;and is a true overseer of his heart,  
&emsp;and a hearer of his tongue.  
<sup>7</sup> Because the spirit of the Lord has filled[^4] the world,  
&emsp;and that which holds all things together knows what is said.  
<sup>8</sup> Therefore no one who utters unrighteous things will be unseen;  
&emsp;neither will Justice, when it convicts, pass him by.  
<sup>9</sup> For in his counsels the ungodly will be searched out,  
&emsp;and the sound of his words will come to the Lord to bring his lawless deeds to conviction;  
<sup>10</sup> because a jealous ear listens to all things,  
&emsp;and the noise of murmurings is not hidden.  
<sup>11</sup> Beware then of unprofitable murmuring,  
&emsp;and keep your tongue from slander;  
&emsp;because no secret utterance will go on its way void,  
&emsp;and a lying mouth destroys a soul.  
<sup>12</sup> Don’t court death in the error of your life.  
&emsp;Don’t draw destruction upon yourselves by the works of your hands;  
&emsp;<sup>13</sup> because God didn’t make death,  
&emsp;neither does he delight when the living perish.  
<sup>14</sup> For he created all things that they might have being.  
&emsp;The generative powers of the world are wholesome,  
&emsp;and there is no poison of destruction in them,  
&emsp;nor has Hades[^5] royal dominion upon earth;  
<sup>15</sup> for righteousness is immortal,  
&emsp;<sup>16</sup> but ungodly men by their hands and their words summon death;  
&emsp;deeming him a friend they[^6] pined away.  
They made a covenant with him,  
&emsp;because they are worthy to belong with him.

## Wisdom of Solomon 2

<sup>1</sup> For they said[^7] within themselves, with unsound reasoning,  
&emsp;“Our life is short and sorrowful.  
&emsp;There is no healing when a man comes to his end,  
&emsp;and no one was ever known who[^8] was released from Hades.  
<sup>2</sup> Because we were born by mere chance,  
&emsp;and hereafter we will be as though we had never been,  
&emsp;because the breath in our nostrils is smoke,  
&emsp;and reason is a spark kindled by the beating of our heart,  
<sup>3</sup> which being extinguished, the body will be turned into ashes,  
&emsp;and the spirit will be dispersed as thin air.  
<sup>4</sup> Our name will be forgotten in time.  
&emsp;No one will remember our works.  
&emsp;Our life will pass away as the traces of a cloud,  
&emsp;and will be scattered as is a mist,  
&emsp;when it is chased by the rays of the sun,  
&emsp;and[^9] overcome by its heat.  
<sup>5</sup> For our allotted time is the passing of a shadow,  
&emsp;and our end doesn’t retreat,  
&emsp;because it is securely sealed, and no one[^10] turns it back.

<sup>6</sup> “Come therefore and let’s enjoy the good things that exist.  
&emsp;Let’s use the creation earnestly as in our youth.  
<sup>7</sup> Let’s fill ourselves with costly wine and perfumes,  
&emsp;and let no spring flower pass us by.  
<sup>8</sup> Let’s crown ourselves with rosebuds before they wither.  
<sup>9</sup> Let none of us go without his share in our proud revelry.  
&emsp;Let’s leave tokens of mirth everywhere,  
&emsp;because this is our portion, and this is our lot.  
<sup>10</sup> Let’s oppress the righteous poor.  
&emsp;Let’s not spare the widow,  
&emsp;nor regard the gray hair of the old man.  
<sup>11</sup> But let our strength be a law of righteousness;  
&emsp;for that which is weak is proven useless.  
<sup>12</sup> But let’s lie in wait for the righteous man,  
&emsp;because he annoys us,  
&emsp;is contrary to our works,  
&emsp;reproaches us with sins against the law,  
&emsp;and charges us with sins against our training.  
<sup>13</sup> He professes to have knowledge of God,  
&emsp;and calls himself a child of the Lord.  
<sup>14</sup> He became to us a reproof of our thoughts.  
<sup>15</sup> He is grievous to us even to look at,  
&emsp;because his life is unlike other men’s,  
&emsp;and his paths are strange.  
<sup>16</sup> We were regarded by him as something worthless,  
&emsp;and he abstains from our ways as from uncleanness.  
&emsp;He calls the latter end of the righteous happy.  
&emsp;He boasts that God is his father.  
<sup>17</sup> Let’s see if his words are true.  
&emsp;Let’s test what will happen at the end of his life.  
<sup>18</sup> For if the righteous man is God’s son, he will uphold him,  
&emsp;and he will deliver him out of the hand of his adversaries.  
<sup>19</sup> Let’s test him with insult and torture,  
&emsp;that we may find out how gentle he is,  
&emsp;and test his patience.  
<sup>20</sup> Let’s condemn him to a shameful death,  
&emsp;for he will be protected, according to his words.”

<sup>21</sup> Thus they reasoned, and they were led astray;  
&emsp;for their wickedness blinded them,  
<sup>22</sup> and they didn’t know the mysteries of God,  
&emsp;neither did they hope for wages of holiness,  
&emsp;nor did they discern that there is a prize for blameless souls.  
<sup>23</sup> Because God created man for incorruption,  
&emsp;and made him an image of his own everlastingness;  
<sup>24</sup> but death entered into the world by the envy of the devil,  
&emsp;and those who belong to him experience it.

## Wisdom of Solomon 3

<sup>1</sup> But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God,  
&emsp;and no torment will touch them.  
<sup>2</sup> In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to have died.  
&emsp;Their departure was considered a disaster,  
&emsp;<sup>3</sup> and their travel away from us ruin,  
&emsp;but they are in peace.  
<sup>4</sup> For even if in the sight of men they are punished,  
&emsp;their hope is full of immortality.  
<sup>5</sup> Having borne a little chastening, they will receive great good;  
&emsp;because God tested them, and found them worthy of himself.  
<sup>6</sup> He tested them like gold in the furnace,  
&emsp;and he accepted them as a whole burnt offering.  
<sup>7</sup> In the time of their visitation they will shine.  
&emsp;They will run back and forth like sparks among stubble.  
<sup>8</sup> They will judge nations and have dominion over peoples.  
&emsp;The Lord will reign over them forever.  
&emsp;<sup>9</sup> Those who trust him will understand truth.  
&emsp;The faithful will live with him in love,  
&emsp;because grace and mercy are with his chosen ones.

<sup>10</sup> But the ungodly will be punished even as their reasoning deserves,  
&emsp;those who neglected righteousness and revolted from the Lord;  
&emsp;<sup>11</sup> for he who despises wisdom and discipline is miserable.  
Their hope is void and their toils unprofitable.  
&emsp;Their works are useless.  
<sup>12</sup> Their wives are foolish and their children are wicked.  
&emsp;<sup>13</sup> Their descendants are cursed.  
For the barren woman who is undefiled is happy,  
&emsp;she who has not conceived in transgression.  
&emsp;She will have fruit when God examines souls.  
<sup>14</sup> So is the eunuch which has done no lawless deed with his hands,  
&emsp;nor imagined wicked things against the Lord;  
for a precious gift will be given to him for his faithfulness,  
&emsp;and a delightful inheritance in the Lord’s sanctuary.  
<sup>15</sup> For good labors have fruit of great renown.  
&emsp;The root of understanding can’t fail.  
<sup>16</sup> But children of adulterers will not come to maturity.  
&emsp;The seed of an unlawful union will vanish away.  
<sup>17</sup> For if they live long, they will not be esteemed,  
&emsp;and in the end, their old age will be without honor.  
<sup>18</sup> If they die young, they will have no hope,  
&emsp;nor consolation in the day of judgment.  
<sup>19</sup> For the end of an unrighteous generation is always grievous.

## Wisdom of Solomon 4

<sup>1</sup> It is better to be childless with virtue,  
&emsp;for immortality is in the memory of virtue,  
&emsp;because it is recognized both before God and before men.  
<sup>2</sup> When it is present, people imitate it.  
&emsp;They long after it when it has departed.  
Throughout all time it marches, crowned in triumph,  
&emsp;victorious in the competition for the prizes that are undefiled.  
<sup>3</sup> But the multiplying brood of the ungodly will be of no profit,  
&emsp;and their illegitimate offshoots won’t take deep root,  
&emsp;nor will they establish a sure hold.  
<sup>4</sup> For even if they grow branches and flourish for a season,  
&emsp;standing unsure, they will be shaken by the wind.  
&emsp;They will be uprooted by the violence of winds.  
<sup>5</sup> Their branches will be broken off before they come to maturity.  
&emsp;Their fruit will be useless,  
&emsp;never ripe to eat, and fit for nothing.  
<sup>6</sup> For unlawfully conceived children are witnesses of wickedness  
&emsp;against parents when they are investigated.

<sup>7</sup> But a righteous man, even if he dies before his time, will be at rest.  
<sup>8</sup> For honorable old age is not that which stands in length of time,  
&emsp;nor is its measure given by number of years,  
&emsp;<sup>9</sup> but understanding is gray hair to men,  
&emsp;and an unspotted life is ripe old age.

<sup>10</sup> Being found well-pleasing to God, someone was loved.  
&emsp;While living among sinners he was transported.  
<sup>11</sup> He was caught away, lest evil should change his understanding,  
&emsp;or guile deceive his soul.  
<sup>12</sup> For the fascination of wickedness obscures the things which are good,  
&emsp;and the whirl of desire perverts an innocent mind.  
<sup>13</sup> Being made perfect quickly,  
&emsp;he filled a long time;  
&emsp;<sup>14</sup> for his soul was pleasing to the Lord.  
&emsp;Therefore he hurried out of the midst of wickedness.  
<sup>15</sup> But the peoples saw and didn’t understand,  
&emsp;not considering this, that grace and mercy are with his chosen,  
&emsp;and that he visits his holy ones;  
<sup>16</sup> but a righteous man who is dead will condemn the ungodly who are living,  
&emsp;and youth who is quickly perfected will condemn the many years of an unrighteous man’s old age.  
<sup>17</sup> For the ungodly will see a wise man’s end,  
&emsp;and won’t understand what the Lord planned for him,  
&emsp;and why he safely kept him.  
<sup>18</sup> They will see, and they will despise;  
&emsp;but the Lord will laugh them to scorn.  
After this, they will become a dishonored carcass  
&emsp;and a reproach among the dead forever;  
<sup>19</sup> because he will dash them speechless to the ground,  
&emsp;and will shake them from the foundations.  
They will lie utterly waste.  
&emsp;They will be in anguish  
&emsp;and their memory will perish.

<sup>20</sup> They will come with coward fear when their sins are counted.  
&emsp;Their lawless deeds will convict them to their face.

## Wisdom of Solomon 5

<sup>1</sup> Then the righteous man will stand in great boldness  
&emsp;before the face of those who afflicted him,  
&emsp;and those who make his labors of no account.  
<sup>2</sup> When they see him, they will be troubled with terrible fear,  
&emsp;and will be amazed at the marvel of salvation.  
<sup>3</sup> They will speak among themselves repenting,  
&emsp;and for distress of spirit they will groan,  
&emsp;“This was he whom we used to hold in derision,  
&emsp;as a parable of reproach.  
<sup>4</sup> We fools considered his life madness,  
&emsp;and his end without honor.  
<sup>5</sup> How was he counted among sons of God?  
&emsp;How is his lot among saints?  
<sup>6</sup> Truly we went astray from the way of truth.  
&emsp;The light of righteousness didn’t shine for us.  
&emsp;The sun didn’t rise for us.  
<sup>7</sup> We[^11] took our fill of the paths of lawlessness and destruction.  
&emsp;We traveled through trackless deserts,  
&emsp;but we didn’t know the Lord’s way.  
<sup>8</sup> What did our arrogance profit us?  
&emsp;What good have riches and boasting brought us?

<sup>9</sup> Those things all passed away as a shadow,  
&emsp;like a rumor that runs by,  
&emsp;<sup>10</sup> like a ship passing through the billowy water,  
&emsp;which, when it has gone by, there is no trace to be found,  
&emsp;no pathway of its keel in the waves.  
<sup>11</sup> Or it is like when a bird flies through the air,  
&emsp;no evidence of its passage is found,  
&emsp;but the light wind, lashed with the stroke of its pinions,  
&emsp;and torn apart with the violent rush of the moving wings, is passed through.  
&emsp;Afterwards no sign of its coming remains.  
<sup>12</sup> Or it is like when an arrow is shot at a mark,  
&emsp;the air it divided closes up again immediately,  
&emsp;so that men don’t know where it passed through.  
<sup>13</sup> So we also, as soon as we were born, ceased to be;  
&emsp;and we had no sign of virtue to show,  
&emsp;but we were utterly consumed in our wickedness.”  
<sup>14</sup> Because the hope of the ungodly man is like chaff carried by the wind,  
&emsp;and[^12] as[^13] foam vanishing before a tempest;  
&emsp;and is scattered like smoke by the wind,  
&emsp;and passes by as the remembrance of a guest who stays just a day.

<sup>15</sup> But the righteous live forever.  
&emsp;Their reward is in the Lord,  
&emsp;and the care for them with the Most High.  
<sup>16</sup> Therefore they will receive the crown of royal dignity  
&emsp;and the diadem of beauty from the Lord’s hand,  
because he will cover them with his right hand,  
&emsp;and he will shield them with his arm.  
<sup>17</sup> He will take his zeal as complete armor,  
&emsp;and will make the whole creation his weapons to punish his enemies:  
<sup>18</sup> He will put on righteousness as a breastplate,  
&emsp;and will wear impartial judgment as a helmet.  
<sup>19</sup> He will take holiness as an invincible shield.  
&emsp;<sup>20</sup> He will sharpen stern wrath for a sword.  
&emsp;The universe will go with him to fight against his frenzied foes.  
<sup>21</sup> Shafts of lightning will fly with true aim.  
&emsp;They will leap to the mark from the clouds, as from a well-drawn bow.  
<sup>22</sup> Hailstones full of wrath will be hurled as from a catapult.  
&emsp;The water of the sea will be angered against them.  
&emsp;Rivers will sternly overwhelm them.  
<sup>23</sup> A mighty wind will encounter them.  
&emsp;It will winnow them away like a tempest.  
So lawlessness will make all the land desolate.  
&emsp;Their evil-doing will overturn the thrones of princes.

## Wisdom of Solomon 6

<sup>1</sup> Hear therefore, you kings, and understand.  
&emsp;Learn, you judges of the ends of the earth.  
&emsp;<sup>2</sup> Give ear, you rulers who have dominion over many people,  
&emsp;and make your boast[^14] in multitudes of nations,  
<sup>3</sup> because your dominion was given to you from the Lord,  
&emsp;and your sovereignty from the Most High.  
He will search out your works,  
&emsp;and will inquire about your plans,  
<sup>4</sup> because being officers of his kingdom, you didn’t judge rightly,  
&emsp;nor did you keep the law,  
&emsp;nor did you walk according to God’s counsel.  
<sup>5</sup> He will come upon you awfully and swiftly,  
&emsp;because a stern judgment comes on those who are in high places.  
<sup>6</sup> For the man of low estate may be pardoned in mercy,  
&emsp;but mighty men will be mightily tested.  
<sup>7</sup> For the Sovereign Lord of all will not be impressed with anyone,  
&emsp;neither will he show deference to greatness;  
because it is he who made both small and great,  
&emsp;and cares about them all;  
<sup>8</sup> but the scrutiny that comes upon the powerful is strict.  
<sup>9</sup> Therefore, my words are to you, O princes,  
&emsp;that you may learn wisdom and not fall away.  
<sup>10</sup> For those who have kept the things that are holy in holiness will be made holy.  
&emsp;Those who have been taught them will find what to say in defense.  
<sup>11</sup> Therefore set your desire on my words.  
&emsp;Long for them, and you princes will be instructed.

<sup>12</sup> Wisdom is radiant and doesn’t fade away;  
&emsp;and is easily seen by those who love her,  
&emsp;and found by those who seek her.  
<sup>13</sup> She anticipates those who desire her, making herself known.  
<sup>14</sup> He who rises up early to seek her won’t have difficulty,  
&emsp;for he will find her sitting at his gates.  
&emsp;<sup>15</sup> For to think upon her is perfection of understanding,  
&emsp;and he who watches for her will quickly be free from care;  
<sup>16</sup> because she herself goes around, seeking those who are worthy of her,  
&emsp;and in their paths she appears to them graciously,  
&emsp;and in every purpose she meets them.  
<sup>17</sup> For her true beginning is desire for instruction;  
&emsp;and desire for instruction is love.  
<sup>18</sup> And love is observance of her laws.  
&emsp;To give heed to her laws confirms immortality.  
<sup>19</sup> Immortality brings closeness to God.  
&emsp;<sup>20</sup> So then desire for wisdom promotes to a kingdom.

<sup>21</sup> If therefore you delight in thrones and sceptres, you princes of peoples,  
&emsp;honor wisdom, that you may reign forever.  
<sup>22</sup> But what wisdom is, and how she came into being, I will declare.  
&emsp;I won’t hide mysteries from you;  
&emsp;but I will explore from her first beginning,  
&emsp;bring the knowledge of her into clear light,  
&emsp;and I will not pass by the truth.  
<sup>23</sup> Indeed, I won’t travel with consuming envy,  
&emsp;because envy will have no fellowship with wisdom.  
<sup>24</sup> But a multitude of wise men is salvation to the world,  
&emsp;and an understanding king is stability for his people.  
<sup>25</sup> Therefore be instructed by my words, and you will profit.

## Wisdom of Solomon 7

<sup>1</sup> I myself am also[^15] mortal, like everyone else,  
&emsp;and am a descendant of one formed first and born of the earth.  
<sup>2</sup> I molded into flesh in the time of ten months in my mother’s womb,  
&emsp;being compacted in blood from the seed of man and pleasure of marriage.  
<sup>3</sup> I also, when I was born, drew in the common air,  
&emsp;and fell upon the kindred earth,  
uttering, like all, for my first voice, the same cry.  
<sup>4</sup> I was nursed with care in swaddling clothes.  
<sup>5</sup> For no king had a different beginning,  
&emsp;<sup>6</sup> but all men have one entrance into life, and a common departure.

<sup>7</sup> For this cause I prayed, and understanding was given to me.  
&emsp;I asked, and a spirit of wisdom came to me.  
<sup>8</sup> I preferred her before sceptres and thrones.  
&emsp;I considered riches nothing in comparison to her.  
<sup>9</sup> Neither did I liken to her any priceless gem,  
&emsp;because all gold in her presence is a little sand,  
&emsp;and silver will be considered as clay before her.  
<sup>10</sup> I loved her more than health and beauty,  
&emsp;and I chose to have her rather than light,  
&emsp;because her bright shining is never laid to sleep.  
<sup>11</sup> All good things came to me with her,  
&emsp;and innumerable riches are in her hands.  
<sup>12</sup> And I rejoiced over them all because wisdom leads them;  
&emsp;although I didn’t know that she was their mother.  
<sup>13</sup> As I learned without guile, I impart without grudging.  
&emsp;I don’t hide her riches.  
<sup>14</sup> For she is a treasure for men that doesn’t fail,  
&emsp;and those who use it obtain friendship with God,  
&emsp;commended by the gifts which they present through discipline.

<sup>15</sup> But may God grant that I may speak his judgment,  
&emsp;and to conceive thoughts worthy of what has been given me;  
&emsp;because he is one who guides even wisdom and who corrects the wise.  
<sup>16</sup> For both we and our words are in his hand,  
&emsp;with all understanding and skill in various crafts.  
<sup>17</sup> For he himself gave me an unerring knowledge of the things that are,  
&emsp;to know the structure of the universe and the operation of the elements;  
&emsp;<sup>18</sup> the beginning, end, and middle of times;  
&emsp;the alternations of the solstices and the changes of seasons;  
&emsp;<sup>19</sup> the circuits of years and the positions of stars;  
&emsp;<sup>20</sup> the natures of living creatures and the raging of wild beasts;  
&emsp;the violence of[^16] winds and the thoughts of men;  
&emsp;the diversities of plants and the virtues of roots.  
<sup>21</sup> All things that are either secret or manifest I learned,  
&emsp;<sup>22</sup> for wisdom, that is the architect of all things, taught me.

For there is in her a spirit that is quick to understand, holy,  
&emsp;unique, manifold, subtle, freely moving, clear in utterance, unpolluted,  
&emsp;distinct, invulnerable, loving what is good, keen, unhindered,  
&emsp;<sup>23</sup> beneficent, loving toward man, steadfast, sure, free from care, all-powerful, all-surveying,  
&emsp;and penetrating through all spirits that are quick to understand, pure, most subtle.  
<sup>24</sup> For wisdom is more mobile than any motion.  
&emsp;Yes, she pervades and penetrates all things by reason of her purity.  
<sup>25</sup> For she is a breath of the power of God,  
&emsp;and a pure emanation of the glory of the Almighty.  
Therefore nothing defiled can find entrance into her.  
<sup>26</sup> For she is a reflection of everlasting light,  
&emsp;an unspotted mirror of the working of God,  
&emsp;and an image of his goodness.  
<sup>27</sup> Although she is one, she has power to do all things.  
&emsp;Remaining in herself, she renews all things.  
From generation to generation passing into holy souls,  
&emsp;she makes friends of God and prophets.  
&emsp;<sup>28</sup> For God loves nothing as much as one who dwells with wisdom.  
<sup>29</sup> For she is fairer than the sun,  
&emsp;and above all the constellations of the stars.  
&emsp;She is better than light.  
<sup>30</sup> For daylight yields to night,  
&emsp;but evil does not prevail against wisdom.

## Wisdom of Solomon 8

<sup>1</sup> But she reaches from one end to the other with full strength,  
&emsp;and orders all things well.

<sup>2</sup> I loved her and sought her from my youth.  
&emsp;I sought to take her for my bride.  
&emsp;I became enamoured by her beauty.  
<sup>3</sup> She glorifies her noble birth by living with God.  
&emsp;The Sovereign Lord of all loves her.  
<sup>4</sup> For she is initiated into the knowledge of God,  
&emsp;and she chooses his works.  
<sup>5</sup> But if riches are a desired possession in life,  
&emsp;what is richer than wisdom, which makes all things?  
<sup>6</sup> And if understanding is effective,  
&emsp;who more than[^17] wisdom is an architect of the things that exist?  
<sup>7</sup> If a man loves righteousness,  
&emsp;the fruits of wisdom’s labor[^18] are virtues,  
&emsp;for she teaches soberness, understanding, righteousness, and courage.  
&emsp;There is nothing in life more profitable for people than these.  
<sup>8</sup> And if anyone longs for wide experience,  
&emsp;she knows the things of old, and infers the things to come.  
&emsp;She understands subtleties of speeches and interpretations of dark sayings.  
&emsp;She foresees signs and wonders, and the issues of seasons and times.

<sup>9</sup> Therefore I determined to take her to live with me,  
&emsp;knowing that she is one who would give me good counsel,  
&emsp;and encourage me in cares and grief.  
<sup>10</sup> Because of her, I will have glory among multitudes,  
&emsp;and honor in the sight of elders, though I am young.  
<sup>11</sup> I will be found keen when I give judgment.  
&emsp;I will be admired in the presence of rulers.  
<sup>12</sup> When I am silent, they will wait for me.  
&emsp;When I open my lips, they will heed what I say.  
&emsp;If I continue speaking, they will put their hands on their mouths.  
<sup>13</sup> Because of her, I will have immortality,  
&emsp;and leave behind an eternal memory to those who come after me.  
<sup>14</sup> I will govern peoples.  
&emsp;Nations will be subjected to me.  
<sup>15</sup> Dreaded monarchs will fear me when they hear of me.  
&emsp;Among the people, I will show myself to be good, and courageous in war.  
<sup>16</sup> When I come into my house, I will find rest with her.  
&emsp;For conversation with her has no bitterness,  
&emsp;and living with her has no pain, but gladness and joy.  
<sup>17</sup> When I considered these things in myself,  
&emsp;and thought in my heart how immortality is in kinship to wisdom,  
<sup>18</sup> and in her friendship is good delight,  
&emsp;and in the labors of her hands is wealth that doesn’t fail,  
&emsp;and understanding is in her companionship,  
&emsp;and great renown in having fellowship with her words,  
&emsp;I went about seeking how to take her to myself.  
<sup>19</sup> Now I was a clever child, and received a good soul.  
&emsp;<sup>20</sup> Or rather, being good, I came into an undefiled body.  
<sup>21</sup> But perceiving that I could not otherwise possess wisdom unless God gave her to me—  
&emsp;yes, and to know and understand by whom the grace is given—  
&emsp;I pleaded with the Lord and implored him, and with my whole heart I said:

## Wisdom of Solomon 9

<sup>1</sup> “O God of my ancestors and Lord of mercy,  
&emsp;who made all things by your word;  
<sup>2</sup> and by your wisdom you formed man,  
&emsp;that he should have dominion over the creatures that were made by you,  
<sup>3</sup> and rule the world in holiness and righteousness,  
&emsp;and execute judgment in uprightness of soul,  
&emsp;<sup>4</sup> give me wisdom, her who sits by you on your thrones.  
Don’t reject me from among your[^19] servants,  
&emsp;<sup>5</sup> because I am your servant and the son of your handmaid,  
&emsp;a weak and short-lived man,  
&emsp;with little power to understand judgment and laws.  
<sup>6</sup> For even if a man is perfect among the sons of men,  
&emsp;if the wisdom that comes from you is not with him, he will count for nothing.  
<sup>7</sup> You chose me to be king of your people,  
&emsp;and a judge for your sons and daughters.  
<sup>8</sup> You gave a command to build a sanctuary on your holy mountain,  
&emsp;and[^20] an altar in the city where you live,  
&emsp;a copy of the holy tent which you prepared from the beginning.  
<sup>9</sup> Wisdom is with you and knows your works,  
&emsp;and was present when you were making the world,  
&emsp;and understands what is pleasing in your eyes,  
&emsp;and what is right according to your commandments.  
<sup>10</sup> Send her from the holy heavens,  
&emsp;and ask her to come from the throne of your glory,  
&emsp;that being present with me she may work,  
&emsp;and I may learn what pleases you well.  
<sup>11</sup> For she knows all things and understands,  
&emsp;and she will guide me prudently in my actions.  
&emsp;She will guard me in her glory.  
<sup>12</sup> So my works will be acceptible.  
&emsp;I will judge your people righteously,  
&emsp;and I will be worthy of my father’s[^21] throne.  
<sup>13</sup> For what man will know the counsel of God?  
&emsp;Or who will conceive what the Lord wills?  
<sup>14</sup> For the thoughts of mortals are unstable,  
&emsp;and our plans are prone to fail.  
<sup>15</sup> For a corruptible body weighs down the soul.  
&emsp;The earthy tent burdens a mind that is full of cares.  
<sup>16</sup> We can hardly guess the things that are on earth,  
&emsp;and we find the things that are close at hand with labor;  
&emsp;but who has traced out the things that are in the heavens?  
<sup>17</sup> Who gained knowledge of your counsel, unless you gave wisdom,  
&emsp;and sent your holy spirit from on high?  
<sup>18</sup> It was thus that the ways of those who are on earth were corrected,  
&emsp;and men were taught the things that are pleasing to you.  
&emsp;They were saved through wisdom.”

## Wisdom of Solomon 10

<sup>1</sup> Wisdom[^22] guarded to the end the first formed father of the world, who was created alone,  
&emsp;and delivered him out of his own transgression,  
<sup>2</sup> and gave him strength to rule over all things.  
<sup>3</sup> But when an unrighteous man fell away from her in his anger,  
&emsp;he perished himself in the rage with which he killed his brother.  
<sup>4</sup> When for his cause the earth was drowning with a flood,  
wisdom again saved it,  
guiding the righteous man’s course by a paltry piece of wood.

<sup>5</sup> Moreover, when nations consenting together in wickedness had been confounded,  
&emsp;wisdom[^23] knew the righteous man, and preserved him blameless to God,  
&emsp;and kept him strong when his heart yearned toward his child.

<sup>6</sup> While the ungodly were perishing, wisdom[^24] delivered a righteous man,  
&emsp;when he fled from the fire that descended out of heaven on the five cities.  
<sup>7</sup> To whose wickedness a smoking waste still witnesses,  
&emsp;and plants bearing fair fruit that doesn’t ripen,  
&emsp;a disbelieving soul has a memorial: a standing pillar of salt.  
<sup>8</sup> For having passed wisdom by,  
&emsp;not only were they disabled from recognising the things which are good,  
&emsp;but they also left behind them for their life a monument of their folly,  
&emsp;to the end that where they stumbled, they might fail even to be unseen;  
<sup>9</sup> but wisdom delivered those who waited on her out of troubles.

<sup>10</sup> When a righteous man was a fugitive from a brother’s wrath,[^25] wisdom guided him in straight paths.  
&emsp;She showed him God’s kingdom, and gave him knowledge of holy things.  
&emsp;She prospered him in his toils, and multiplied the fruits of his labor.  
<sup>11</sup> When in their covetousness men dealt harshly with him,  
&emsp;she stood by him and made him rich.  
<sup>12</sup> She guarded him from enemies,  
&emsp;and she kept him safe from those who lay in wait.  
&emsp;Over his severe conflict, she watched as judge,  
&emsp;that he might know that godliness is more powerful than every one.

<sup>13</sup> When a righteous man was sold,[^26] wisdom didn’t forsake him,  
&emsp;but she delivered him from sin.  
&emsp;She went down with him into a dungeon,  
<sup>14</sup> and in bonds she didn’t depart from him,  
&emsp;until she brought him the sceptre of a kingdom,  
&emsp;and authority over those that dealt like a tyrant with him.  
&emsp;She also showed those who had mockingly accused him to be false,  
&emsp;and gave him eternal glory.

<sup>15</sup> Wisdom[^27] delivered a holy people and a blameless seed from a nation of oppressors.  
<sup>16</sup> She entered into the soul of a servant of the Lord,  
&emsp;and withstood terrible kings in wonders and signs.  
<sup>17</sup> She rendered to holy men a reward of their toils.  
&emsp;She guided them along a marvelous way,  
&emsp;and became to them a covering in the day-time,  
&emsp;and a starry flame through the night.  
<sup>18</sup> She brought them over the Red sea,  
&emsp;and led them through much water;  
<sup>19</sup> but she drowned their enemies,  
&emsp;and she cast them up from the bottom of the deep.  
<sup>20</sup> Therefore the righteous plundered the ungodly,  
&emsp;and they sang praise to your holy name, O Lord,  
&emsp;and extolled with one accord your hand that fought for them,  
<sup>21</sup> because wisdom opened the mouth of the mute,  
and made the tongues of babes to speak clearly.

## Wisdom of Solomon 11

<sup>1</sup> Wisdom prospered their works in the hand of a holy prophet.

<sup>2</sup> They traveled through a desert without inhabitant,  
&emsp;and they pitched their tents in trackless regions.  
<sup>3</sup> They withstood enemies and repelled foes.  
<sup>4</sup> They thirsted, and they called upon you,  
&emsp;and water was given to them out of the[^28] flinty rock,  
&emsp;and healing of their thirst out of the hard stone.  
<sup>5</sup> For by what things their foes were punished,  
&emsp;by these they in their need were benefited.  
<sup>6</sup> When enemies were troubled with clotted blood  
&emsp;instead of a river’s ever-flowing fountain,  
<sup>7</sup> to rebuke the decree for the slaying of babies,  
&emsp;you gave them abundant water beyond all hope,  
<sup>8</sup> having shown by the thirst which they had suffered  
&emsp;how you punished the adversaries.  
<sup>9</sup> For when they were tried, although chastened in mercy,  
&emsp;they learned how the ungodly were tormented, being judged with wrath.  
<sup>10</sup> For you tested these as a father admonishing them;  
&emsp;but you searched out those as a stern king condemning them.  
<sup>11</sup> Yes and whether they were far off or near,  
&emsp;they were equally distressed;  
<sup>12</sup> for a double grief seized them,  
&emsp;and a groaning at the memory of things past.  
<sup>13</sup> For when they heard that through their own punishments the others benefited,  
&emsp;they recognized the Lord.  
<sup>14</sup> For him who long before was thrown out and exposed they stopped mocking.  
&emsp;In the end of what happened, they marveled,  
&emsp;having thirsted in another manner than the righteous.

<sup>15</sup> But in return for the senseless imaginings of their unrighteousness,  
&emsp;wherein they were led astray to worship irrational reptiles and wretched vermin,  
&emsp;you sent upon them a multitude of irrational creatures to punish them,  
&emsp;<sup>16</sup> that they might learn that by what things a man sins, by these he is punished.  
<sup>17</sup> For your all-powerful hand  
&emsp;that created the world out of formless matter  
&emsp;didn’t lack means to send upon them a multitude of bears, fierce lions,  
&emsp;<sup>18</sup> or newly-created and unknown wild beasts, full of rage,  
&emsp;either breathing out a blast of fiery breath,  
&emsp;or belching out smoke,  
&emsp;or flashing dreadful sparks from their eyes;  
<sup>19</sup> which had power not only to consume them by their violence,  
&emsp;but to destroy them even by the terror of their sight.  
<sup>20</sup> Yes and without these they might have fallen by a single breath,  
&emsp;being pursued by Justice, and scattered abroad by the breath of your power;  
&emsp;but you arranged all things by measure, number, and weight.

<sup>21</sup> For to be greatly strong is yours at all times.  
&emsp;Who could withstand the might of your arm?  
<sup>22</sup> Because the whole world before you is as a grain in a balance,  
&emsp;and as a drop of dew that comes down upon the earth in the morning.  
<sup>23</sup> But you have mercy on all men, because you have power to do all things,  
&emsp;and you overlook the sins of men to the end that they may repent.  
<sup>24</sup> For you love all things that are,  
&emsp;and abhor none of the things which you made;  
&emsp;For you never would have formed anything if you hated it.  
<sup>25</sup> How would anything have endured unless you had willed it?  
&emsp;Or that which was not called by you, how would it have been preserved?  
<sup>26</sup> But you spare all things, because they are yours,  
&emsp;O Sovereign Lord, you who love the living.

## Wisdom of Solomon 12

<sup>1</sup> For your incorruptible spirit is in all things.  
<sup>2</sup> Therefore you convict little by little those who fall from the right way,  
&emsp;and, putting them in remembrance by the things wherein they sin, you admonish them,  
&emsp;that escaping from their wickedness they may believe in you, O Lord.

<sup>3</sup> For truly the old inhabitants of your holy land,  
&emsp;<sup>4</sup> hating them because they practiced detestable works of enchantments and unholy rites—  
&emsp;<sup>5</sup> merciless slaughters of children  
&emsp;and sacrificial banquets of men’s flesh and of blood—  
&emsp;<sup>6</sup> allies in an impious fellowship,  
&emsp;and murderers of their own helpless babes,  
&emsp;it was your counsel to destroy by the hands of our fathers;  
&emsp;<sup>7</sup> that the land which in your sight is most precious of all  
&emsp;might receive a worthy colony of God’s servants.[^29]  
<sup>8</sup> Nevertheless you even spared these as men,  
&emsp;and you sent hornets[^30] as forerunners of your army,  
&emsp;to cause them to perish little by little.  
<sup>9</sup> Not that you were unable to subdue the ungodly under the hand of the righteous in battle,  
&emsp;or by terrible beasts or by a stern word to make away with them at once,  
<sup>10</sup> but judging them little by little you gave them a chance to repent,  
&emsp;not being ignorant that their nature by birth was evil,  
&emsp;their wickedness inborn,  
&emsp;and that their manner of thought would never be changed.  
<sup>11</sup> For they were a cursed seed from the beginning.  
&emsp;It wasn’t through fear of any that you left them unpunished for their sins.

<sup>12</sup> For who will say, “What have you done?”  
&emsp;Or “Who will withstand your judgment?”  
Who will accuse you for the perishing of nations which you caused?  
&emsp;Or who will come and stand before you as an avenger for unrighteous men?  
<sup>13</sup> For there isn’t any God beside you that cares for all,  
&emsp;that you might show that you didn’t judge unrighteously.  
<sup>14</sup> No king or prince will be able to confront you  
&emsp;about those whom you have punished.  
<sup>15</sup> But being righteous, you rule all things righteously,  
&emsp;deeming it a thing alien from your power  
&emsp;to condemn one who doesn’t deserve to be punished.  
<sup>16</sup> For your strength is the source of righteousness,  
&emsp;and your sovereignty over all makes you to forbear all.  
<sup>17</sup> For when men don’t believe that you are perfect in power, you show your strength,  
&emsp;and in dealing with those who think this, you confuse their boldness.  
<sup>18</sup> But you, being sovereign in strength, judge in gentleness,  
&emsp;and with great forbearance you govern us;  
&emsp;for the power is yours whenever you desire it.

<sup>19</sup> But you taught your people by such works as these,  
&emsp;how the righteous must be kind.  
You made your sons to have good hope,  
&emsp;because you give repentance when men have sinned.  
<sup>20</sup> For if on those who were enemies of your servants[^31] and deserving of death,  
&emsp;you took vengeance with so great deliberation and indulgence,  
&emsp;giving them times and opportunities when they might escape from their wickedness,  
<sup>21</sup> with how great care you judged your sons,  
&emsp;to whose fathers you gave oaths and covenants of good promises!  
<sup>22</sup> Therefore while you chasten us, you scourge our enemies ten thousand times more,  
&emsp;to the intent that we may ponder your goodness when we judge,  
&emsp;and when we are judged may look for mercy.

<sup>23</sup> Therefore also the unrighteous that lived in a life of folly,  
&emsp;you tormented through their own abominations.  
<sup>24</sup> For truly they went astray very far in the ways of error,  
&emsp;Taking as gods those animals[^32] which even among their enemies were held in dishonor,  
&emsp;deceived like foolish babes.  
<sup>25</sup> Therefore, as to unreasoning children, you sent your judgment to mock them.  
<sup>26</sup> But those who would not be admonished by mild correction  
&emsp;will experience the deserved judgment of God.  
<sup>27</sup> For through the sufferings they were indignant of,  
&emsp;being punished in these creatures which they supposed to be gods,  
&emsp;they saw and recognized as the true God him whom they previously refused to know.  
&emsp;Therefore also the result of extreme condemnation came upon them.

## Wisdom of Solomon 13

<sup>1</sup> For truly all men who had no perception of God were foolish by nature,  
&emsp;and didn’t gain power to know him who exists from the good things that are seen.  
&emsp;They didn’t recognize the architect from his works.  
<sup>2</sup> But they thought that either fire, or wind, or swift air,  
&emsp;or circling stars, or raging water, or luminaries of heaven  
&emsp;were gods that rule the world.  
<sup>3</sup> If it was through delight in their beauty that they took them to be gods,  
&emsp;let them know how much better their Sovereign Lord is than these,  
&emsp;for the first author of beauty created them.  
<sup>4</sup> But if it was through astonishment at their power and influence,  
&emsp;then let them understand from them how much more powerful he who formed them is.  
<sup>5</sup> For from the greatness of the beauty of created things,  
&emsp;mankind forms the corresponding perception of their Maker.[^33]  
<sup>6</sup> But yet for these men there is but small blame,  
&emsp;for they too perhaps go astray  
&emsp;while they are seeking God and desiring to find him.  
<sup>7</sup> For they diligently search while living among his works,  
&emsp;and they trust their sight that the things that they look at are beautiful.  
<sup>8</sup> But again even they are not to be excused.  
<sup>9</sup> For if they had power to know so much,  
&emsp;that they should be able to explore the world,  
&emsp;how is it that they didn’t find the Sovereign Lord sooner?

<sup>10</sup> But they were miserable, and their hopes were in dead things,  
&emsp;who called them gods which are works of men’s hands,  
&emsp;gold and silver, skillfully made, and likenesses of animals,  
&emsp;or a useless stone, the work of an ancient hand.  
<sup>11</sup> Yes and some[^34] woodcutter might saw down a tree that is easily moved,  
&emsp;skillfully strip away all its bark,  
&emsp;and fashion it in attractive form, make a useful vessel to serve his life’s needs.  
<sup>12</sup> Burning the scraps from his handiwork to cook his food,  
&emsp;he eats his fill.  
<sup>13</sup> Taking a discarded scrap which served no purpose,  
&emsp;a crooked piece of wood and full of knots,  
&emsp;he carves it with the diligence of his idleness,  
&emsp;and shapes it by the skill of his idleness.  
He shapes it in the image of a man,  
&emsp;<sup>14</sup> or makes it like some worthless animal,  
&emsp;smearing it with something red, painting it red,  
&emsp;and smearing over every stain in it.  
<sup>15</sup> Having made a worthy chamber for it,  
&emsp;he sets it in a wall, securing it with iron.  
<sup>16</sup> He plans for it that it may not fall down,  
&emsp;knowing that it is unable to help itself  
&emsp;(for truly it is an image, and needs help).  
<sup>17</sup> When he makes his prayer concerning goods and his marriage and children,  
&emsp;he is not ashamed to speak to that which has no life.  
<sup>18</sup> Yes, for health, he calls upon that which is weak.  
&emsp;For life, he implores that which is dead.  
&emsp;For aid, he supplicates that which has no experience.  
&emsp;For a good journey, he asks that which can’t so much as move a step.  
<sup>19</sup> And for profit in business and good success of his hands,  
&emsp;he asks ability from that which has hands with no ability.

## Wisdom of Solomon 14

<sup>1</sup> Again, one preparing to sail, and about to journey over raging waves,  
&emsp;calls upon a piece of wood more fragile than the vessel that carries him.  
<sup>2</sup> For the hunger for profit planned it,  
&emsp;and wisdom was the craftsman who built it.  
<sup>3</sup> Your providence, O Father, guides it along,  
&emsp;because even in the sea you gave a way,  
&emsp;and in the waves a sure path,  
<sup>4</sup> showing that you can save out of every danger,  
&emsp;that even a man without skill may put to sea.  
<sup>5</sup> It is your will that the works of your wisdom should not be ineffective.  
&emsp;Therefore men also entrust their lives to a little piece of wood,  
&emsp;and passing through the surge on a raft come safely to land.  
<sup>6</sup> For[^35] in the old time also, when proud giants were perishing,  
&emsp;the hope of the world, taking refuge on a raft,  
&emsp;your hand guided the seed of generations of the race of men.  
<sup>7</sup> For blessed is wood through which comes righteousness;  
&emsp;<sup>8</sup> but the idol made with hands is accursed, itself and he that made it;  
&emsp;because his was the working, and the corruptible thing was called a god.  
<sup>9</sup> For both the ungodly and his ungodliness are alike hateful to God;  
&emsp;<sup>10</sup> for truly the deed will be punished together with him who committed it.  
&emsp;<sup>11</sup> Therefore also there will be a visitation among the idols of the nation,  
&emsp;because, though formed of things which God created, they were made an abomination,  
&emsp;stumbling blocks to the souls of men,  
&emsp;and a snare to the feet of the foolish.

<sup>12</sup> For the devising of idols was the beginning of fornication,  
&emsp;and the invention of them the corruption of life.  
<sup>13</sup> For they didn’t exist from the beginning, and they won’t exist forever.  
<sup>14</sup> For by the boastfulness of men they entered into the world,  
&emsp;and therefore a speedy end was planned for them.

<sup>15</sup> For a father worn with untimely grief,  
&emsp;making an image of the child quickly taken away,  
&emsp;now honored him as a god which was then a dead human being,  
&emsp;and delivered to those that were under him mysteries and solemn rites.  
<sup>16</sup> Afterward the ungodly custom, in process of time grown strong, was kept as a law,  
&emsp;and the engraved images received worship by the commandments of princes.  
<sup>17</sup> And when men could not honor them in presence because they lived far off,  
&emsp;imagining the likeness from afar,  
&emsp;they made a visible image of the king whom they honored,  
&emsp;that by their zeal they might flatter the absent as if present.

<sup>18</sup> But worship was raised to a yet higher pitch, even by those who didn’t know him,  
&emsp;urged forward by the ambition of the architect;  
<sup>19</sup> for he, wishing perhaps to please his ruler,  
&emsp;used his art to force the likeness toward a greater beauty.  
<sup>20</sup> So the multitude, allured by reason of the grace of his handiwork,  
&emsp;now consider an object of devotion him that a little before was honored as a man.  
<sup>21</sup> And this became an ambush,  
&emsp;because men, in bondage either to calamity or to tyranny,  
&emsp;invested stones and stocks with the Name that shouldn’t be shared.

<sup>22</sup> Afterward it was not enough for them to go astray concerning the knowledge of God,  
&emsp;but also, while they live in a great war of ignorance, they call a multitude of evils peace.  
<sup>23</sup> For either slaughtering children in solemn rites, or celebrating secret mysteries,  
&emsp;or holding frenzied revels of strange customs,  
<sup>24</sup> no longer do they guard either life or purity of marriage,  
&emsp;but one brings upon another either death by treachery, or anguish by adultery.  
<sup>25</sup> And all things confusedly are filled with blood and murder, theft and deceit,  
&emsp;corruption, faithlessness, tumult, perjury,  
<sup>26</sup> confusion about what is good, forgetfulness of favors,  
&emsp;ingratitude for benefits,  
&emsp;defiling of souls, confusion of sex,  
&emsp;disorder in marriage, adultery and wantonness.  
&emsp;<sup>27</sup> For the worship of idols that may not be named [^36]  
&emsp;is a beginning and cause and end of every evil.  
<sup>28</sup> For their worshipers either make merry to madness, or prophesy lies,  
&emsp;or live unrighteously, or lightly commit perjury.  
<sup>29</sup> For putting their trust in lifeless idols,  
&emsp;when they have sworn a wicked oath, they expect not to suffer harm.  
<sup>30</sup> But on both counts, the just doom will pursue them,  
&emsp;because they had evil thoughts of God by giving heed to idols,  
&emsp;and swore unrighteously in deceit through contempt for holiness.  
<sup>31</sup> For it is not the power of things by which men swear,  
&emsp;but it is the just penalty for those who sin  
&emsp;that always visits the transgression of the unrighteous.

## Wisdom of Solomon 15

<sup>1</sup> But you, our God, are gracious and true,  
&emsp;patient, and in mercy ordering all things.  
<sup>2</sup> For even if we sin, we are yours, knowing your dominion;  
&emsp;but we will not sin, knowing that we have been accounted yours.  
<sup>3</sup> For to be acquainted with you is[^37] perfect righteousness,  
&emsp;and to know your dominion is the root of immortality.  
<sup>4</sup> For we weren’t led astray by any evil plan of men’s,  
&emsp;nor yet by painters’ fruitless labor,  
&emsp;a form stained with varied colors,  
<sup>5</sup> the sight of which leads fools into[^38] lust.  
&emsp;Their desire is for the breathless form of a dead image.  
<sup>6</sup> Lovers of evil things, and worthy of such hopes,  
&emsp;are those who make, desire, and worship them.

<sup>7</sup> For a potter, kneading soft earth,  
&emsp;laboriously molds each article for our service.  
He fashions out of the same clay  
&emsp;both the vessels that minister to clean uses, and those of a contrary sort,  
&emsp;all in like manner.  
What shall be the use of each article of either sort,  
&emsp;the potter is the judge.  
<sup>8</sup> Also, laboring to an evil end, he molds a vain god out of the same clay,  
&emsp;he who, having but a little before been made of earth,  
&emsp;after a short space goes his way to the earth out of which he was taken,  
&emsp;when he is required to give back the[^39] soul which was lent him.  
<sup>9</sup> However he has anxious care,  
&emsp;not because his powers must fail,  
&emsp;nor because his span of life is short;  
But he compares himself with goldsmiths and silversmiths,  
&emsp;and he imitates molders in[^40] brass,  
&emsp;and considers it great that he molds counterfeit gods.  
<sup>10</sup> His heart is ashes.  
&emsp;His hope is of less value than earth.  
&emsp;His life is of less honor than clay,  
<sup>11</sup> because he was ignorant of him who molded him,  
&emsp;and of him that inspired into him[^41] an active[^42] soul,  
&emsp;and breathed into him a vital spirit.  
<sup>12</sup> But[^43] he accounted our life to be a game,  
&emsp;and our[^44] lifetime a festival for profit;  
for, he says, one must get gain however one can, even if it is by evil.  
<sup>13</sup> For this man, beyond all others, knows that he sins,  
&emsp;out of earthy matter making brittle vessels and engraved images.  
<sup>14</sup> But most foolish and more miserable than a baby,  
&emsp;are the enemies of your people, who oppressed them;  
<sup>15</sup> because they even considered all the idols of the nations to be gods,  
&emsp;which have neither the use of eyes for seeing,  
&emsp;nor nostrils for drawing breath,  
&emsp;nor ears to hear,  
&emsp;nor fingers for handling,  
&emsp;and their feet are helpless for walking.  
<sup>16</sup> For a man made them,  
&emsp;and one whose own spirit is borrowed molded them;  
&emsp;for no one has power as a man to mold a god like himself.  
<sup>17</sup> But, being mortal, he makes a dead thing by the work of lawless hands;  
&emsp;for he is better than the objects of his worship,  
&emsp;since he indeed had life, but they never did.

<sup>18</sup> Yes, and they worship the creatures that are most hateful,  
&emsp;for, being compared as to lack of sense, these are worse than all others;  
<sup>19</sup> Neither, as seen beside other creatures, are they beautiful, so that one should desire them,  
&emsp;but they have escaped both the praise of God and his blessing.

## Wisdom of Solomon 16

<sup>1</sup> For this cause, they were deservedly punished through creatures like those which they worship,  
&emsp;and tormented through a multitude of vermin.  
<sup>2</sup> Instead of this punishment, you, giving benefits to your people,  
&emsp;prepared quails for food,  
a delicacy to satisfy the desire of their appetite,  
<sup>3</sup> to the end that your enemies, desiring food,  
&emsp;might for the hideousness of the creatures sent among them,  
&emsp;loathe even the necessary appetite;  
but these, your people, having for a short time suffered lack,  
&emsp;might even partake of delicacies.  
<sup>4</sup> For it was necessary that inescapable lack should come upon those oppressors,  
&emsp;but that to these it should only be showed how their enemies were tormented.  
<sup>5</sup> For even when terrible raging of wild beasts came upon your people,  
&emsp;and they were perishing by the bites of crooked serpents,  
&emsp;your wrath didn’t continue to the uttermost;  
<sup>6</sup> but for admonition were they troubled for a short time,  
&emsp;having a token of salvation  
&emsp;to put them in remembrance of the commandment of your law;

<sup>7</sup> for he who turned toward it was not saved because of that which was seen,  
&emsp;but because of you, the Savior of all.  
<sup>8</sup> Yes, and in this you persuaded our enemies  
&emsp;that you are he who delivers out of every evil.  
<sup>9</sup> For the bites of locusts and flies truly killed them.  
&emsp;No healing for their life was found,  
&emsp;because they were worthy to be punished by such things.  
<sup>10</sup> But your children weren’t overcome by the very fangs of venomous dragons,  
&emsp;for your mercy passed by where they were and healed them.  
<sup>11</sup> For they were bitten to put them in remembrance of your oracles,  
&emsp;and were quickly saved, lest, falling into deep forgetfulness,  
&emsp;they should become unable to respond to your kindness.  
<sup>12</sup> For truly it was neither herb nor poultice that cured them,  
&emsp;but your word, O Lord, which heals all people.  
<sup>13</sup> For you have authority over life and death,  
&emsp;and you lead down to the gates of Hades, and lead up again.  
<sup>14</sup> But though a man kills by his wickedness,  
&emsp;he can’t retrieve the spirit that has departed  
&emsp;or release the imprisoned soul.

<sup>15</sup> But it is not possible to escape your hand;  
<sup>16</sup> for ungodly men, refusing to know you, were scourged in the strength of your arm,  
&emsp;pursued with strange rains and hails and relentless storms,  
&emsp;and utterly consumed with fire.  
<sup>17</sup> For, what was most marvelous,  
&emsp;in the water which quenches all things, the fire burned hotter;  
&emsp;for the world fights for the righteous.  
<sup>18</sup> For at one time the flame was restrained,  
&emsp;that it might not burn up the creatures sent against the ungodly,  
&emsp;but that these themselves as they looked might see that they were chased by the judgment of God.  
<sup>19</sup> At another time even in the midst of water it burns more intensely than fire,  
&emsp;that it may destroy the produce of an unrighteous land.  
<sup>20</sup> Instead of these things, you gave your people angels’ food to eat,  
&emsp;and you provided ready-to-eat bread for them from heaven without toil,  
&emsp;having the virtue of every pleasant flavor,  
&emsp;and agreeable to every taste.  
<sup>21</sup> For your nature showed your sweetness toward your children,  
&emsp;while that bread, serving the desire of the eater,  
&emsp;changed itself according to every man’s choice.  
<sup>22</sup> But snow and ice endured fire, and didn’t melt,  
&emsp;that people might know that fire was destroying the fruits of the enemies,  
&emsp;burning in the hail and flashing in the rains;  
<sup>23</sup> and that this fire, again, in order that righteous people may be nourished,  
&emsp;has even forgotten its own power.  
<sup>24</sup> For the creation, ministering to you, its maker,  
&emsp;strains its force against the unrighteous for punishment  
&emsp;and in kindness, slackens it on behalf of those who trust in you.  
<sup>25</sup> Therefore at that time also, converting itself into all forms,  
&emsp;it ministered to your all-nourishing bounty,  
&emsp;according to the desire of those who had need,  
<sup>26</sup> that your children, whom you loved, O Lord, might learn  
&emsp;that it is not the growth of crops that nourishes a man,  
&emsp;but that your word preserves those who trust you.  
<sup>27</sup> For that which was not destroyed by fire,  
&emsp;melted away when it was simply warmed by a faint sunbeam,  
<sup>28</sup> that it might be known that we must rise before the sun to give you thanks,  
&emsp;and must pray to you at the dawning of the light;  
<sup>29</sup> for the hope of the unthankful will melt as the winter’s hoar frost,  
&emsp;and will flow away as water that has no use.

## Wisdom of Solomon 17

<sup>1</sup> For your judgments are great, and hard to interpret;  
&emsp;therefore undisciplined souls went astray.  
<sup>2</sup> For when lawless men had supposed that they held a holy nation in their power,  
&emsp;they, prisoners of darkness, and bound in the fetters of a long night,  
&emsp;kept close beneath their roofs,  
&emsp;lay exiled from the eternal providence.  
<sup>3</sup> For while they thought that they were unseen in their secret sins,  
&emsp;they were divided from one another by a dark curtain of forgetfulness,  
&emsp;stricken with terrible awe, and very troubled by apparitions.  
<sup>4</sup> For neither did the dark recesses that held them guard them from fears,  
&emsp;but terrifying sounds rang around them,  
&emsp;and dismal phantoms appeared with unsmiling faces.  
<sup>5</sup> And no power of fire prevailed to give light,  
&emsp;neither were the brightest flames of the stars strong enough to illuminate that gloomy night;  
<sup>6</sup> but only the glimmering of a self-kindled fire appeared to them, full of fear.  
&emsp;In terror, they considered the things which they saw  
&emsp;to be worse than that sight, on which they could not gaze.  
<sup>7</sup> The mockeries of their magic arts were powerless, now,  
&emsp;and a shameful rebuke of their boasted understanding:  
<sup>8</sup> For those who promised to drive away terrors and disorders from a sick soul,  
&emsp;these were sick with a ludicrous fearfulness.  
<sup>9</sup> For even if no troubling thing frighted them,  
&emsp;yet, scared with the creeping of vermin and hissing of serpents,  
<sup>10</sup> they perished trembling in fear,  
&emsp;refusing even to look at the air, which could not be escaped on any side.  
<sup>11</sup> For wickedness, condemned by a witness within, is a coward thing,  
&emsp;and, being pressed hard by conscience, always has added forecasts of the worst.  
&emsp;<sup>12</sup> For fear is nothing else but a surrender of the help which reason offers;  
&emsp;<sup>13</sup> and from within, the expectation of being less  
&emsp;prefers ignorance of the cause that brings the torment.  
<sup>14</sup> But they, all through the night which was powerless indeed,  
&emsp;and which came upon them out of the recesses of powerless Hades,  
&emsp;sleeping the same sleep,  
&emsp;<sup>15</sup> now were haunted by monstrous apparitions,  
&emsp;and now were paralyzed by their soul’s surrendering;  
&emsp;for sudden and unexpected fear came upon them.  
<sup>16</sup> So then whoever it might be, sinking down in his place,  
&emsp;was kept captive, shut up in that prison which was not barred with iron;  
<sup>17</sup> for whether he was a farmer, or a shepherd,  
&emsp;or a laborer whose toils were in the wilderness,  
&emsp;he was overtaken, and endured that inescapable sentence;  
&emsp;for they were all bound with one chain of darkness.  
<sup>18</sup> Whether there was a whistling wind,  
&emsp;or a melodious sound of birds among the spreading branches,  
&emsp;or a measured fall of water running violently,  
&emsp;<sup>19</sup> or a harsh crashing of rocks hurled down,  
&emsp;or the swift course of animals bounding along unseen,  
&emsp;or the voice of wild beasts harshly roaring,  
&emsp;or an echo rebounding from the hollows of the mountains,  
&emsp;all these things paralyzed them with terror.  
<sup>20</sup> For the whole world was illuminated with clear light,  
&emsp;and was occupied with unhindered works,  
&emsp;<sup>21</sup> while over them alone was spread a heavy night,  
&emsp;an image of the darkness that should afterward receive them;  
&emsp;but to themselves, they were heavier than darkness.

## Wisdom of Solomon 18

<sup>1</sup> But for your holy ones there was great light.  
&emsp;Their enemies, hearing their voice but not seeing their form,  
&emsp;counted it a happy thing that they too had suffered,  
<sup>2</sup> yet for that they do not hurt them, though wronged by them before, they are thankful;  
&emsp;and because they had been at variance with them, they begged for pardon.  
<sup>3</sup> Therefore you provided a burning pillar of fire,  
&emsp;to be a guide for your people’s unknown journey,  
&emsp;and a harmless sun for their glorious exile.  
<sup>4</sup> For the Egyptians well deserved to be deprived of light and imprisoned by darkness,  
&emsp;they who had imprisoned your children,  
&emsp;through whom the incorruptible light of the law was to be given to the race of men.

<sup>5</sup> After they had taken counsel to kill the babes of the holy ones,  
&emsp;and when a single child had been abandoned and saved to convict them of their sin,  
&emsp;you took away from them their multitude of children,  
&emsp;and destroyed all their army together in a mighty flood.  
<sup>6</sup> Our fathers were made aware of that night beforehand,  
&emsp;that, having sure knowledge, they might be cheered by the oaths which they had trusted.  
<sup>7</sup> Salvation of the righteous and destruction of the enemies was expected by your people.  
<sup>8</sup> For as you took vengeance on the adversaries,  
&emsp;by the same means, calling us to yourself, you glorified us.  
<sup>9</sup> For holy children of good men offered sacrifice in secret,  
&emsp;and with one consent they agreed to the covenant of the divine law,  
&emsp;that they would partake alike in the same good things and the same perils,  
&emsp;the fathers already leading the sacred songs of praise.  
<sup>10</sup> But the discordant cry of the enemies echoed back,  
&emsp;and a pitiful voice of lamentation for children was spread abroad.  
<sup>11</sup> Both servant and master were punished with the same just doom,  
&emsp;and the commoner suffering the same as king;  
<sup>12</sup> Yes, they all together, under one form of death,  
&emsp;had corpses without number.  
For the living were not sufficient even to bury them,  
&emsp;Since at a single stroke, their most cherished offspring was consumed.  
<sup>13</sup> For while they were disbelieving all things by reason of the enchantments,  
&emsp;upon the destruction of the firstborn they confessed the people to be God’s children.  
<sup>14</sup> For while peaceful silence wrapped all things,  
&emsp;and night in her own swiftness was half spent,  
<sup>15</sup> your all-powerful word leaped from heaven, from the royal throne,  
&emsp;a stern warrior, into the midst of the doomed land,  
<sup>16</sup> bearing as a sharp sword your authentic commandment,  
&emsp;and standing, it filled all things with death,  
&emsp;and while it touched the sky it stood upon the earth.  
<sup>17</sup> Then immediately apparitions in dreams terribly troubled them,  
&emsp;and unexpected fears came upon them.  
<sup>18</sup> And each, one thrown here half dead, another there,  
&emsp;made known why he was dying;  
<sup>19</sup> for the dreams, disturbing them, forewarned them of this,  
&emsp;that they might not perish without knowing why they were afflicted.

<sup>20</sup> The experience of death also touched the righteous,  
&emsp;and a multitude were destroyed in the wilderness,  
&emsp;but the wrath didn’t last long.  
<sup>21</sup> For a blameless man hurried to be their champion,  
&emsp;bringing the weapon of his own ministry,  
&emsp;prayer, and the atoning sacrifice of incense.  
He withstood the indignation and set an end to the calamity,  
&emsp;showing that he was your servant.  
<sup>22</sup> And he overcame the anger,  
&emsp;not by strength of body, not by force of weapons,  
&emsp;but by his word, he subdued the avenger  
&emsp;by bringing to remembrance oaths and covenants made with the fathers.  
<sup>23</sup> For when the dead had already fallen in heaps one upon another,  
&emsp;he intervened and stopped the wrath,  
&emsp;and cut off its way to the living.  
<sup>24</sup> For the whole world was pictured on his long robe,  
&emsp;and the glories of the fathers were upon the engraving of the four rows of precious stones,  
&emsp;and your majesty was upon the diadem on his head.  
<sup>25</sup> The destroyer yielded to these, and they feared;  
&emsp;for it was enough only to test the wrath.

## Wisdom of Solomon 19

<sup>1</sup> But indignation without mercy came upon the ungodly to the end;  
&emsp;for God also foreknew their future,  
<sup>2</sup> how, having changed their minds to let your people go,  
&emsp;and having sped them eagerly on their way,  
&emsp;they would change their minds and pursue them.  
<sup>3</sup> For while they were yet in the midst of their mourning,  
&emsp;and lamenting at the graves of the dead,  
&emsp;they made another foolish decision,  
&emsp;and pursued as fugitives those whom they had begged to leave and driven out.  
<sup>4</sup> For the doom which they deserved was drawing them to this end,  
&emsp;and it made them forget the things that had happened to them,  
&emsp;that they might fill up the punishment which was yet lacking from their torments,  
&emsp;<sup>5</sup> and that your people might journey on by a marvelous road,  
&emsp;but they themselves might find a strange death.

<sup>6</sup> For the whole creation, each part in its diverse kind, was made new again,  
&emsp;complying with your commandments,  
&emsp;that your servants might be kept unharmed.  
<sup>7</sup> Then the cloud that overshadowed the camp was seen,  
&emsp;and dry land rising up out of what had been water,  
&emsp;out of the Red sea an unhindered highway,  
&emsp;and a grassy plain out of the violent surge,  
&emsp;<sup>8</sup> by which they passed over with all their army,  
&emsp;these who were covered with your hand,  
&emsp;having seen strange marvels.  
<sup>9</sup> For like horses they roamed at large,  
&emsp;and they skipped about like lambs,  
&emsp;praising you, O Lord, who was their deliverer.  
<sup>10</sup> For they still remembered the things that happened in the time of their sojourning,  
&emsp;how instead of bearing cattle, the land brought forth lice,  
&emsp;and instead of fish, the river spewed out a multitude of frogs.  
<sup>11</sup> But afterwards, they also saw a new kind of birds,  
&emsp;when, led on by desire, they asked for luxurious dainties;  
<sup>12</sup> for, to comfort them, quails came up for them from the sea.

<sup>13</sup> Punishments came upon the sinners,  
&emsp;not without the signs that were given beforehand by the violence of the thunder,  
&emsp;for they justly suffered through their own wickednesses,  
&emsp;for the hatred which they practiced toward guests was grievous indeed.  
<sup>14</sup> For while the others didn’t receive the strangers when they came to them,  
&emsp;the Egyptians made slaves of guests who were their benefactors.  
<sup>15</sup> And not only so, but while punishment of some sort will come upon the former,  
&emsp;since they received as enemies those who were aliens;  
&emsp;<sup>16</sup> because these first welcomed with feastings,  
&emsp;and then afflicted with dreadful toils,  
&emsp;those who had already shared with them in the same rights.  
<sup>17</sup> And moreover they were stricken with loss of sight  
&emsp;(even as were those others at the righteous man’s doors),  
&emsp;when, being surrounded with yawning darkness,  
&emsp;they each looked for the passage through his own door.

<sup>18</sup> For as the notes of a lute vary the character of the rhythm,  
&emsp;even so the elements, changing their order one with another,  
&emsp;continuing always in its sound,  
&emsp;as may clearly be conjectured from the sight of the things that have happened.  
<sup>19</sup> For creatures of the dry land were turned into creatures of the waters,  
&emsp;and creatures that swim moved upon the land.  
<sup>20</sup> Fire kept the mastery of its own power in water,  
&emsp;and water forgot its quenching nature.  
<sup>21</sup> On the contrary, flames didn’t consume flesh of perishable creatures that walked among them,  
&emsp;neither did they melt the crystalline grains of ambrosial food that were melted easily.

<sup>22</sup> For in all things, O Lord, you magnified your people,  
&emsp;and you glorified them and didn’t lightly regard them,  
&emsp;standing by their side in every time and place.
